% ==============================================================================
\section{An Iterated-Greedy Heuristic with Neighbourhood Descent}
\label{sec:heuristic}
% ==============================================================================

The MILP of \S\ref{sec:milp} is exact but its size grows quickly: the
partition variables $z^-,\,z^+,\,b^B,\,b^C$ contribute
$O\!\left(|\calR|^2 \cdot |\calA| \cdot (N_{\max}+1)\right)$ binaries, so
proving optimality becomes intractable well before the fleet sizes of
operational interest (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).  We therefore develop a
metaheuristic, \textsc{IGVND}, that couples an Iterated-Greedy outer loop
with a first-improvement local search over three neighbourhoods around a
problem-specific decoder, and complements it with an \emph{alignment
polish} on a small per-aircraft timing shift that lets a rear aircraft's
access land exactly on a boundary of the front it blocks.  The design
draws on the NEH constructive heuristic, Iterated Greedy, and Variable
Neighbourhood Search~\citep{Nawaz1983,Ruiz2007,Hansen2001,Mladenovic1997}.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:igvnd_principle}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The central idea, recurrent in the scheduling literature the method draws
on, is to \emph{separate the expensive combinatorial decision from the
cheap timing decision}.  A solution is represented by a compact
\emph{combinatorial state} $(\pi, \sigma)$, where $\pi : \calR \to \calP$
is the position assignment and $\sigma$ is a global priority order over
the aircraft.  All search is conducted on this small state.  An inner,
deterministic \emph{decoder} maps a state $(\pi, \sigma)$ to concrete job
start and finish times, classifies every blocking access into its mode
(\S\ref{sec:problem}), and returns the objective value~\eqref{eq:milpobj};
we write $z(\pi,\sigma)$ for that value.  The decoder is the oracle that
prices a combinatorial state.  The only timing quantity the search ever
touches is a \emph{shift} $\theta_r = (d_r, g_r)$ per aircraft --- a delay
$d_r \ge 0$ of its earliest start and an idle $g_r \ge 0$ inserted before
its last job --- which is zero everywhere by default, is honoured only by
the manoeuvre-aware decoder, and is searched only by a dedicated polish
(\S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}); we write $z_1(\pi,\sigma,\theta)$ when the
shift matters and $z_1(\pi,\sigma)$ for the zero shift.

The method is built from six blocks, each detailed in a subsection below:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item a \textbf{decoder} (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_decoder}) in two regimes ---
        a zero-movement regime that is feasible by construction, and a
        manoeuvre-aware regime that may spend Mode~B/Mode~C movements
        when they reduce the objective;
  \item a \textbf{construction scheme} (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_construction})
        built on two deterministic priority rules plus a rank-biased
        randomised shuffle that gives every further restart a distinct
        seed;
  \item an exact-filtered \textbf{local-search descent}
        (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_vnd}) that refines a state to a local optimum;
  \item an \textbf{Iterated-Greedy} loop (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_ig}) that
        perturbs and re-optimises around the incumbent;
  \item an \textbf{alignment polish} (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}) that
        searches the per-aircraft shift so that rear accesses land on
        front boundaries, the structure the certified optima use; and
  \item a \textbf{multi-start} driver with a feasibility safety net
        (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_safety}).
\end{enumerate}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:igvnd} states the method at a high level.  Restarts are
drawn \emph{until the time budget is exhausted}: each restart takes a seed
order (deterministic for the first two, biased-randomised afterwards),
optimises it first in the zero-movement regime to obtain a
guaranteed-feasible \emph{floor}, and then polishes it in the
manoeuvre-aware regime --- where the state additionally carries the shift
$\theta$, started from zero --- with the two phases splitting the
restart's time slice evenly; the polished schedule is retained only if an
independent feasibility check certifies it and it improves the floor.  A
single nest-stretch candidate is added when the weights reward it.  The
best certified schedule over all restarts is returned.  Because every
restart ends early once its own search stalls, small instances perform
hundreds of independent restarts within the budget, while larger ones run
two long trajectories --- one per clearance geometry of the zero-movement
decoder --- so that the cheaper descents accumulate into many kicks
instead of being split over restarts that never converge.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\KwData{instance $I$; weights $(W^M, W^D, W^S)$; time budget $T$.}
\KwResult{a feasible schedule.}
preprocess $I$; let $\rho_{\mathrm{NEH}}, \rho_{\mathrm{SLACK}}$ be the two
   deterministic priority rules (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_construction})\;
set the per-restart time cap $\tau = T/8$ if $R \le 10$, $\;T/2$
   otherwise\;
$z^\star \leftarrow +\infty$;\quad $i \leftarrow 1$\;
\While{time remains}{
  allot $\min(\tau,\ \text{remaining time})$ to restart $i$\;
  $\rho \leftarrow \rho_{\mathrm{NEH}}$ if $i{=}1$;\;\quad
     $\rho_{\mathrm{SLACK}}$ if $i{=}2$;\;\quad
     $\textsc{BiasedShuffle}$ of the better-scoring of the two if $i \ge 3$
     \tcp*{Alg.~\ref{alg:construct}}
  $(\pi, \sigma) \leftarrow \textsc{Construct}(\rho)$\;
  $(\pi, \sigma) \leftarrow \textsc{Search}(\pi, \sigma, \textsc{Decode}_0)$;
     \quad $z_{\mathrm{floor}} \leftarrow z_0(\pi, \sigma)$
     \tcp*{local-search descent + IG destroy/rebuild (Alg.~\ref{alg:search}); the zero-movement floor}
  $(\pi', \sigma', \theta') \leftarrow \textsc{Search}(\pi, \sigma, \textsc{Decode}_1)$
     \tcp*{the same search re-run under $\textsc{Decode}_1$ from the zero shift; the manoeuvre-aware polish, now also aligning $\theta$ (Alg.~\ref{alg:polish})}
  \eIf{$z_1(\pi', \sigma', \theta') < z_{\mathrm{floor}}$ \textbf{\emph{and}}
        $\textsc{Decode}_1(\pi', \sigma', \theta')$ passes the feasibility check}{
    $(\sigma^\circ, z^\circ) \leftarrow$ the polished schedule and its value\;
  }{
    $(\sigma^\circ, z^\circ) \leftarrow$ the floor schedule and $z_{\mathrm{floor}}$\;
  }
  \lIf{$z^\circ < z^\star$}{$z^\star \leftarrow z^\circ$; record the schedule}
  $i \leftarrow i + 1$\;
}
\If{$W^S$ dominates the weights \textbf{\emph{and}} $\calA \ne \emptyset$}{
  build the nest-stretch candidate (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_safety})\;
  adopt it if it is feasible and improves $z^\star$\;
}
\Return the recorded schedule attaining $z^\star$\;
\caption{\textsc{IGVND} --- master procedure.}
\label{alg:igvnd}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The decoder}
\label{sec:igvnd_decoder}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Zero-movement regime.}
This regime produces schedules that are feasible by construction with
\emph{no manoeuvres} ($n = 0$).  For every blocking arc it keeps each rear
aircraft's two access instants in Mode~A.  Relative to a front aircraft,
this leaves exactly three admissible placements of a rear aircraft's stay:
entirely \emph{before} the front, entirely \emph{after} it, or
\emph{enclosing} it (the rear enters before the front starts and leaves
after it finishes).  The clearance kept between an access and the front
stay alternates between restarts: even restarts use the checker-exact
geometry, in which touching a stay edge is allowed, and odd restarts keep
a conservative margin of $\eta$, so both landscapes stay reachable.  The third placement
--- \emph{nesting} --- is what makes the regime competitive: a long
aircraft can wrap a shorter one so that two blocking-related aircraft
overlap in time instead of being serialised.  Aircraft sharing a position
are separated by $\varepsilon$; aircraft on non-conflicting positions run
in parallel.  Processing the aircraft in the priority order $\sigma$, the
decoder places each one at the earliest start that respects every
constraint against the already-placed aircraft --- a forbidden-interval
scan that naturally lands in the hole left by the nesting option
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:decode0}).  Jobs are then packed tight, so
$\kappa_j = 0$ for every job and no Mode~B/Mode~C feedback on timing
can arise.  This regime is fast, always feasible, and already optimal on
instances with little or no blocking.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\KwData{state $(\pi, \sigma)$.}
\KwResult{a feasible schedule with $n = 0$, and its value $z_0$.}
$\mathcal{Q} \leftarrow \emptyset$ \tcp*{aircraft already placed}
\ForEach{$r \in \calR$ \emph{in the order} $\sigma$}{
  $\mathcal{F} \leftarrow \emptyset$ \tcp*{forbidden start instants for $r$}
  \ForEach{$r' \in \mathcal{Q}$}{
    \lIf{$\pi(r') = \pi(r)$}{add to $\mathcal{F}$ the band that violates the
         $\varepsilon$ separation from $r'$}
    \ForEach{$(p, p') \in \calA$ \emph{with} $\{\pi(r), \pi(r')\} = \{p, p'\}$}{
      add to $\mathcal{F}$ the bands that would place an access of the rear
         aircraft strictly inside the front stay (within $\eta$ of it on
         odd restarts)\;
      \tcp*[l]{the admissible residue is: before, after, or enclosing}
    }
  }
  $s_r \leftarrow \min\{\, t \ge E_r : t \notin \mathcal{F} \,\}$\;
  lay the jobs of $r$ back-to-back from $s_r$ with $\kappa_j = 0$\;
  $\mathcal{Q} \leftarrow \mathcal{Q} \cup \{r\}$\;
}
\Return the schedule and $z_0 = W^M m + W^D \sum_r v^D_r$ \tcp*{$n = 0$}
\caption{$\textsc{Decode}_0$ --- zero-movement decoder.}
\label{alg:decode0}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Manoeuvre-aware regime.}
When the weights make manoeuvres worth their cost, the schedule can be
compressed further by \emph{spending} them.  The positions are processed
deepest-rear first, so that when a front aircraft is laid out the access
instants of all the rear aircraft it blocks are already fixed.  A front
aircraft's start is then chosen to minimise its \emph{local} contribution
to the objective over a candidate set that always contains the
zero-movement placements as well as starts that align a job interior over
an access (inviting a cheap Mode~C) or a job end on an access (inviting a
cheap Mode~B); the local cost of a start is evaluated by a forward sweep
that classifies every exposed access and prices the resulting manoeuvres
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:decode1}).  The sweep applies the closed boundary
semantics of the problem statement exactly as the checker does: an access
that coincides with the front's start or finish is Mode~A (the front is
just entering or leaving its slot), an access that coincides with a job
end is Mode~B through the gap that follows that job, and a gap is opened
after the job whose end is \emph{nearest} to the access --- never after an
earlier one, which would waste idle time and extend the stay.  Only
accesses strictly inside a job's $\eta$-margin, or inside a
non-interruptible job, are rejected.  Because the zero-movement
placements remain candidates, this regime can only ever \emph{add} the
manoeuvre option --- it never discards a feasible no-movement schedule.

Processing rears before fronts has one blind spot: a rear aircraft is laid
at its earliest feasible start and can never \emph{react} to where its
front ends up.  The certified optima of \S\ref{sec:experiments}, however,
frequently delay a rear aircraft, or stretch its stay with idle before its
last job, precisely so that its exit (or entry) coincides with a boundary
of the front it blocks --- the front's stay start or finish, or a job end
--- where the access is Mode~A or a cheap Mode~B instead of a Mode~C
interruption.  The decoder therefore honours the per-aircraft shift
$\theta_r = (d_r, g_r)$ introduced in \S\ref{sec:igvnd_principle}: the
lower bound on $r$'s start becomes $E_r + d_r$, and an idle of $g_r$ is
inserted before its last job, so that the finish $f_r$ moves by $g_r$
while the earlier jobs stay where they were.  With $\theta = 0$ the
decoder is exactly the one just described, so the floor and the
guarantees around it are untouched; how $\theta$ is searched is the
subject of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}.

The cost of one manoeuvre-aware decode is polynomial.  Let
$A \le 2(|\calR| - 1)$ bound the exposed access instants a front aircraft
faces and $N_{\max}$ the longest job chain: each front prices
$O(A\,N_{\max})$ candidate starts, and pricing one start is an
$O(N_{\max} A^2)$ forward sweep (the $\kappa$ fixpoint scans the accesses
once per widening step).  A full $\textsc{Decode}_1$ call over the fleet is
therefore $O(|\calR|^4 N_{\max}^2)$ in the worst case; in practice $A$
counts only the aircraft assigned to the rear endpoints of blocking arcs,
so the effective cost is far below this bound.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\SetKwProg{Fn}{Procedure}{}{}
\SetKwFunction{FPlace}{PlaceFront}
\KwData{state $(\pi, \sigma)$; shift $\theta = (d_r, g_r)_{r \in \calR}$, zero by default.}
\KwResult{a feasible schedule, possibly with manoeuvres, and its value $z_1$.}
\ForEach{position $p \in \calP$ \emph{in order of decreasing blocking depth}}{
  $\phi \leftarrow -\infty$ \tcp*{finish of the previous aircraft at $p$}
  \ForEach{$r$ \emph{assigned to} $p$, \emph{in the order} $\sigma$}{
    $\ell \leftarrow \max\{E_r + d_r,\ \phi + \varepsilon\}$
       \tcp*{the shift delays the earliest start}
    $A \leftarrow$ the access instants of every already-placed rear of $r$\;
    $(s_r, f_r) \leftarrow \FPlace{$r, \ell, A$}$;\quad $\phi \leftarrow f_r$\;
  }
}
\Return the schedule and its value $z_1$\;
\BlankLine
\Fn{\FPlace{$r$, $\ell$, $A$}}{
  $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \{\ell\}
     \cup \{\, \tau - T_r,\ \tau - \eta - T_r,\ \tau,\ \tau - \eta,\ \tau + \eta : \tau \in A \,\}$
     \tcp*{before / after / nest (touching allowed)}
  $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \mathcal{S}
     \cup \{\text{starts aligning a job interior over some } \tau \in A\}$
     \tcp*{invite Mode C}
  $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \mathcal{S}
     \cup \{\text{starts aligning a job end on some } \tau \in A\}$
     \tcp*{invite Mode B}
  $c^\star \leftarrow +\infty$\;
  \ForEach{$s \in \mathcal{S}$ \emph{with} $s \ge \ell$}{
    sweep the jobs of $r$ from $s$, with an idle of $g_r$ before the last
       job, classifying each $\tau \in A$ as
       Mode~A ($\tau$ outside the stay or exactly on its edges), Mode~B
       (open a gap of width $\ge \mu$ after the job whose end is nearest
       to $\tau$; $\tau$ may sit exactly on that end) or Mode~C (extend
       the job by $\delta$); reject $s$ if any $\tau$ cannot be
       classified or falls strictly inside a job's $\eta$-margin\;
    \If{$s$ \emph{is feasible}}{
      $c \leftarrow W^M f_r + W^D \max\{0, f_r - L_r\} + W^S \cdot 2\nu_s$
         \tcp*{$\nu_s$ = manoeuvres at $s$}
      \lIf{$c < c^\star$}{$c^\star \leftarrow c$; keep $s$ as the best start}
    }
  }
  \Return the best start and its finish\;
}
\caption{$\textsc{Decode}_1$ --- manoeuvre-aware decoder.}
\label{alg:decode1}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Construction: two rules and a biased shuffle}
\label{sec:igvnd_construction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each restart builds its seed by greedy insertion under a priority order
$\rho$, so restarts differ by construction and not only by the
perturbation source.  Two deterministic orders cover the objective's two
combinatorial levers: $-T_r$ (the NEH makespan rule --- the bulkiest
aircraft are placed while the schedule is still empty) and the slack
$L_r - E_r - T_r$ (least due-date headroom first, which steers
tight-target aircraft into early slots --- the delay lever).  Every
restart after the second draws its order from a single diversification
mechanism: a \emph{rank-biased geometric shuffle} of whichever
deterministic order scored better, in which the next aircraft is drawn
from the remaining base order with $\Pr(\text{rank } k) \propto
(1-\beta)^k$.  The bias $\beta = 0.3$ was fixed a priori --- it places
roughly two thirds of the probability mass on the first three ranks,
keeping every shuffle close to the logic of its base rule --- and was not
tuned; a sensitivity sweep over $\beta$ is left to future work.  Each
restart thus explores a distinct but still rule-shaped insertion order
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:construct}).  Combined with the open-ended restart
loop of Algorithm~\ref{alg:igvnd}, this gives the search an unlimited
supply of diverse seeds.  The cost of a tentative insertion is the value
returned by the zero-movement decoder restricted to the aircraft committed
so far.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\KwData{a priority order $\rho$; for the shuffle, a base order and the RNG.}
\KwResult{a seed state $(\pi, \sigma)$.}
\textbf{Greedy insertion under $\rho$:}\;
\Indp
  $\pi \leftarrow \emptyset$\;
  \ForEach{$r \in \calR$ \emph{in the order} $\rho$}{
    $\pi(r) \leftarrow \argmin_{p \in \calP}
       z_0\big(\pi \cup \{r \mapsto p\}\big)$
       \tcp*{cheapest position given the committed prefix}
  }
  \Return $(\pi,\ \rho)$\;
\Indm
\BlankLine
\textbf{\textsc{BiasedShuffle} of a base order $\rho_{\mathrm{base}}$:}\;
\Indp
  $\mathit{pool} \leftarrow \rho_{\mathrm{base}}$;\quad $\rho \leftarrow ()$\;
  \While{$\mathit{pool}$ is not empty}{
    draw a rank $k$ with $\Pr(k) \propto (1-\beta)^{k}$
       \tcp*{$\beta = 0.3$: most mass on ranks 0--3}
    move the $\min(k, |\mathit{pool}|-1)$-th element of $\mathit{pool}$
       to the end of $\rho$\;
  }
  \Return $\rho$ \tcp*{then construct greedily under $\rho$}
\Indm
\caption{\textsc{Construct} --- greedy insertion and the biased shuffle.}
\label{alg:construct}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Local search}
\label{sec:igvnd_vnd}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A seed is refined to a local optimum by a first-improvement descent over
three neighbourhoods that act on the combinatorial state and delegate all
timing to the decoder (Algorithm~\ref{alg:vnd}): $N_1$ \emph{reassign}
(move one aircraft to a different position), $N_2$ \emph{relocate} (move
one aircraft to another slot of the priority order $\sigma$), and $N_3$
\emph{swap} (exchange the positions of two aircraft).  Two exact filters
keep the descent cheap without changing the set of local optima it can
reach.  First, under the zero-movement decoder only the aircraft on the
\emph{pusher tree} can improve the objective: the decoder records, for
every aircraft, which already-placed aircraft pushed its start forward,
and a move that touches only aircraft outside the tree rooted at the
makespan and the delayed aircraft leaves every binding start unchanged
(their bands never bound anyone and their delay is zero), so it can only
add constraints.  Under the manoeuvre-aware decoder every aircraft is a
candidate.  Second, relocating an aircraft changes the decode only when its
order relative to an aircraft it interacts with (same position, or a
blocking arc; same position only under $\textsc{Decode}_1$, which
sequences within positions) changes, so $N_2$ visits one representative
slot per such class instead of every slot.  Candidates are scanned in a
random order; the first strictly improving move is applied and the
candidate set is recomputed, until no move improves.  Under
$\textsc{Decode}_1$ the state also carries the shift $\theta$: the descent
prices every move with the shifts the aircraft inherit and never changes
them --- the shift is searched only by the polish of
\S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}, because as a permanent fourth neighbourhood it
multiplied the cost of a non-improving pass about fivefold and starved
the outer loop.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\KwData{state $(\pi, \sigma)$; decoder $\textsc{Decode}_d$.}
\KwResult{a state that is locally optimal for $N_1, N_2, N_3$.}
\Repeat{no candidate has an improving move}{
  $\mathcal{C} \leftarrow$ pusher tree of $\textsc{Decode}_d(\pi, \sigma)$
     if $d = 0$, else $\calR$; shuffle $\mathcal{C}$\;
  \ForEach{$r \in \mathcal{C}$}{
    try, in this order, the $N_1$ moves of $r$, the $N_2$ moves of $r$
       (one slot per distinct-decode class) and the $N_3$ moves of $r$;
       apply the first that strictly lowers $z_d(\pi, \sigma)$ and
       \textbf{break}\;
  }
}
\Return $(\pi, \sigma)$\;
\caption{\textsc{LocalSearch} --- exact-filtered first-improvement descent.}
\label{alg:vnd}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Iterated-Greedy outer loop}
\label{sec:igvnd_ig}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Around the local search runs an Iterated-Greedy loop (Algorithm~\ref{alg:search}).
Each iteration \emph{perturbs} the current state by removing $k$ aircraft
--- with equal probability either uniformly at random or by a delay-weighted
contribution score (lightly randomised), the two together keeping the walk
from anchoring on the same removals --- and \emph{reconstructs} the state by
reinserting each removed aircraft at the position and order-slot that
minimises the decoded objective (again one slot per distinct-decode
class); a removed aircraft loses its shift, which was aligned to a context
the rebuild changes.  The perturbed state is re-optimised by the local
search and accepted as the new current state when it does not worsen the
walk; the best state is tracked separately.  The procedure is
decoder-agnostic: the master procedure calls it once with
$\textsc{Decode}_0$ for the floor and once with $\textsc{Decode}_1$ for
the polish.  Under $\textsc{Decode}_1$ the loop additionally calls the
alignment polish of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_align} at three points
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:search}): every candidate receives the cheap
single-aircraft alignment step before it is judged, a new best receives
the full alignment descent, and the shift kicks run once, on the best
state, when the walk has stalled.  The first of these hooks matters most:
judged with the shifts it inherits --- mostly none --- a candidate that
carries the assignment and order of an optimum can price several times
worse than it is, and was never accepted before the hook existed.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\SetKwProg{Fn}{Procedure}{}{}
\SetKwFunction{FPert}{Perturb}
\KwData{seed $(\pi, \sigma)$; decoder $\textsc{Decode}_d$; deadline.}
\KwResult{the best state found.}
$(\pi, \sigma) \leftarrow \textsc{LocalSearch}(\pi, \sigma, \textsc{Decode}_d)$;\quad
   $\theta \leftarrow 0$\;
\lIf{$d = 1$}{$\theta \leftarrow \textsc{Align}(\pi, \sigma, \theta;\ 3)$
   \tcp*[f]{full alignment descent, Alg.~\ref{alg:polish}}}
\emph{best} $\leftarrow$ \emph{cur} $\leftarrow (\pi, \sigma, \theta)$;\quad
   $t \leftarrow 0$ \tcp*{$t$ = non-improving streak}
\Repeat{the deadline is reached \emph{or} $t \ge t_{\max}$}{
  \emph{cand} $\leftarrow \textsc{LocalSearch}\big(\FPert{\emph{cur}},
     \textsc{Decode}_d\big)$
     \tcp*{\FPert{} clears the shift of the aircraft it removes}
  \lIf{$d = 1$}{align \emph{cand} with the single-aircraft step only,
     $\textsc{Align}(\cdot;\ 1)$ \tcp*[f]{judge it with its shifts aligned}}
  \lIf{$d = 1$ \textbf{\emph{and}} $z_d(\emph{cand}) < z_d(\emph{best})$}{align
     \emph{cand} fully, $\textsc{Align}(\cdot;\ 3)$}
  \lIf{$z_d(\emph{cand}) \le z_d(\emph{cur})$}{\emph{cur} $\leftarrow$ \emph{cand}}
  \eIf{$z_d(\emph{cand}) < z_d(\emph{best})$}{
    \emph{best} $\leftarrow$ \emph{cand};\quad $t \leftarrow 0$\;
  }{
        $t \leftarrow t + 1$\;
  }
  \If{$d = 1$ \textbf{\emph{and}} $t \ge t_{\max}$ \textbf{\emph{and}} time remains}{
    \emph{best}$' \leftarrow \textsc{Polish}(\emph{best})$
       \tcp*{shift kicks, only once the walk has stalled}
    \lIf{$z_1(\emph{best}') < z_1(\emph{best})$}{\emph{best} $\leftarrow$
       \emph{cur} $\leftarrow$ \emph{best}$'$;\quad $t \leftarrow 0$}
  }
}
\Return \emph{best}\;
\BlankLine
\Fn{\FPert{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$}}{
  with probability $\tfrac12$: remove $k$ aircraft uniformly at random;\;
  otherwise: remove the $k$ aircraft of largest score
     $W^D v^D_r + \omega\, T_r$ (lightly randomised)\;
  \ForEach{removed aircraft $r$}{
        $\theta_r \leftarrow (0, 0)$;\;
        reinsert $r$ at the $(\text{position}, \text{slot in } \sigma)$
       minimising the decoded objective, one slot per distinct-decode class\;
  }
  \Return the rebuilt state\;
}
\caption{\textsc{Search} --- Iterated-Greedy loop around the local search.}
\label{alg:search}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Aligning rear aircraft on front boundaries}
\label{sec:igvnd_align}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The shift $\theta$ gives the manoeuvre-aware decoder the structure the
certified optima use (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_decoder}); this subsection
describes how it is searched.  The search is deliberately narrow.  A
shift is only ever proposed to put an access of an aircraft \emph{exactly
on a boundary} of an aircraft it interacts with in the current decoded
schedule, so the candidate set is small and every candidate has a
reason; and it runs as a polish of selected states rather than as a
neighbourhood of the descent.

\paragraph{Proposals.}
For an aircraft $r$ on a blocking position, consider the front aircraft
it interacts with in the current schedule: on every position that blocks
$\pi(r)$, those whose stay overlaps $r$'s, plus the last one finishing
before $r$ and the first one starting after it.  Their \emph{boundaries}
are their stay start and finish, their job starts and ends, and the job
ends they would have if their jobs ran tight from their start --- the
natural ends, which only materialise once a rear exit stops pushing the
front.  The proposals of $r$ are the shifts that place $r$'s exit (by the
stretch $g_r$ or by the delay $d_r$) or $r$'s entry (by $d_r$) exactly on
one of these boundaries, plus the reset $(0,0)$.  Symmetrically, when $r$
is itself a front, the entry and exit of the rear aircraft on the
positions it blocks are added: a front that stays until its rear leaves,
with the two boundaries coinciding, is how the optima wrap a wave of
aircraft along a chain without a single manoeuvre.

\paragraph{Alignment descent.}
$\textsc{Align}$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:polish}) is a first-improvement
descent over the aircraft on blocking positions, in random order, using
proposals only.  Each aircraft escalates through three steps, the next
tried only when the previous finds nothing: (a) a single aligned shift of
$r$; (b) a second alignment of $r$ from each state reached by (a) ---
once $r$ moves the decoder re-places its fronts, so the boundary $r$ must
land on often exists only after a first move; and (c) a first step of $r$
combined with one of the six nearest alignments of a \emph{related}
aircraft $q$ (a front $r$ interacts with, a rear behind $r$, or a sibling
sharing a front with $r$) --- along a chain the shifts cascade, and
siblings wrapping the same front move together in the optima.  Steps (b)
and (c) are capped to the twelve first steps nearest to $r$'s current
shift, and (b) to the six second steps nearest to each.  Escalation is
global: (b) is tried only once no aircraft improves under (a), (c) only
once none improves under (b), and after any improvement the descent
returns to (a).  The end state is a local optimum for all three steps,
but the costly (c) is paid only when the cheap steps are exhausted.  A
level argument stops the escalation early: $\textsc{Align}(\cdot;\ 1)$
runs (a) alone, the cheap form every Iterated-Greedy candidate receives.

\paragraph{Shift kicks.}
The descent only reaches boundaries of the \emph{current} schedule, while
the optimum often needs several aircraft moved together to instants that
become boundaries only once the others have moved --- a valley no single
improving step crosses.  $\textsc{Polish}$ therefore follows the descent
with an iterated local search on the shift alone: it kicks one to three
random aircraft, each to one of its own proposals drawn at random (not
necessarily improving), re-runs the alignment descent, and keeps the
result if it is better; it stops after as many consecutive fruitless
kicks as there are candidate aircraft, or when its budget is spent.  A
polish may use at most a quarter of the time left in the phase, so that
proving a large hub alignment-optimal does not come out of the search
itself.  Within Algorithm~\ref{alg:search} the kicks run only when the
walk has stalled: fired on every new best of a search that is still
improving they only take time from the outer loop.  The whole mechanism
adds one state component and no tunable parameter.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\SetKwProg{Fn}{Procedure}{}{}
\SetKwFunction{FAlign}{Align}
\SetKwFunction{FPolish}{Polish}
\KwData{state $(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$ under $\textsc{Decode}_1$; deepest
   step $L \in \{1, 3\}$; polish budget.}
\KwResult{an aligned shift $\theta$.}
\Fn{\FAlign{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$; $L$}}{
  $\mathit{level} \leftarrow$ (a)\;
  \While{$\mathit{level} \le L$ \textbf{\emph{and}} time remains}{
    $\mathit{found} \leftarrow$ false\;
    \ForEach{aircraft $r$ on a blocking position, in random order}{
      try the proposals of $r$ at $\mathit{level}$: (a) one alignment of
         $r$; (b) a second alignment of $r$ after a first; (c) a first
         alignment of $r$ plus one of a related aircraft $q$\;
      \lIf{one strictly lowers $z_1(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$}{apply it;
         $\mathit{found} \leftarrow$ true; \textbf{break}}
    }
    $\mathit{level} \leftarrow$ (a) if $\mathit{found}$, else the next level\;
  }
  \Return $\theta$\;
}
\BlankLine
\Fn{\FPolish{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$}}{
  $\theta \leftarrow \FAlign{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$; $3$}$;\quad
     $\mathit{fails} \leftarrow 0$\;
  \While{$\mathit{fails} <$ number of candidate aircraft \textbf{\emph{and}}
         budget remains}{
    $\theta' \leftarrow \theta$ with one to three random aircraft each set
       to one of its own proposals, drawn at random\;
    $\theta' \leftarrow \FAlign{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta')$; $3$}$\;
    \lIf{$z_1(\pi, \sigma, \theta') < z_1(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$}{$\theta
       \leftarrow \theta'$;\quad $\mathit{fails} \leftarrow 0$}
    \lElse{$\mathit{fails} \leftarrow \mathit{fails} + 1$}
  }
  \Return $\theta$\;
}
\caption{\textsc{Align} and \textsc{Polish} --- the alignment polish on
   the per-aircraft shift.}
\label{alg:polish}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Multi-start and the feasibility safety net}
\label{sec:igvnd_safety}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The construct-and-improve procedure is restarted until the time budget is
exhausted, and the best certified result is kept.  Each restart is capped at
a time slice of $T/8$ ($R \le 10$) or $T/2$ (larger) and ends early on its
own stale counter, so small instances complete many independent restarts
while larger ones run exactly two long trajectories, one under each
clearance geometry of the zero-movement decoder --- with no
instance-dependent tuning of the restart count.
Because the search is time-limited and hence non-deterministic, a single
run occasionally settles in a poor high-delay basin, and independent
restarts make finding the good basin reliable.

Two safeguards make the method robust.  First, within each restart the
manoeuvre-aware polish is adopted only if an \emph{independent compliance
check} certifies it and it strictly improves the zero-movement floor
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:igvnd}); the method can therefore never return an
infeasible schedule and can never do worse than its zero-movement result,
whatever approximation the manoeuvre-aware decoder makes.  Second, when the
movement-priority profile meets a blocking topology, one further candidate
is built once and folded in by the same best-of / check rule: a
\emph{concentric nest-stretch} schedule over the actual blocking digraph,
written with explicit start times rather than via the earliest-feasible
decode.  The zero-movement optimum often \emph{stretches} an aircraft's
stay with inter-job idle so it can wrap a blocking partner --- a schedule
shape the earliest-feasible decode cannot produce, because it always
prefers placing \emph{before} over \emph{nested} and packs jobs tight.  The
builder groups aircraft into rounds of one per position; within a round,
only the positions that actually block each other receive the concentric
treatment --- along every front$\to$rear arc the rear's stay is stretched
so it wraps the front's stay by $\eta$ on both sides (deepest rear =
outermost shell) --- while unconflicted positions run tight and in
parallel.  Rounds are serialised within the blocking component and simply
chained per position elsewhere; a small beam over four round partitions
(long-first, short-first, earliest-release, round-robin) is tried and the
best kept.  Where the candidate does not help it is dominated and ignored
by the best-of.
