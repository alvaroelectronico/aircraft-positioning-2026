% ==============================================================================
\section{The IGVND metaheuristic}
\label{sec:heuristic}
% ==============================================================================

The MILP of \S\ref{sec:milp} is exact but its size grows quickly: the
partition variables $z^-,\,z^+,\,b^B,\,b^C$ contribute
$O\!\left(|\calR|^2 \cdot |\calA| \cdot (N_{\max}+1)\right)$ binaries,
which limits the instance sizes that can be solved to optimality within
the experimental time budget (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).  We therefore
develop a metaheuristic, \textsc{IGVND}, that couples an Iterated-Greedy
outer loop with a first-improvement local search over three
neighbourhoods around a problem-specific decoder, and complements it with
an \emph{alignment refinement} of a per-aircraft timing shift that places
a rear aircraft's access exactly on a boundary of the front aircraft that
blocks its access.  The design
draws on the NEH constructive heuristic, Iterated Greedy, and Variable
Neighbourhood Search~\citep{Nawaz1983,Ruiz2007,Hansen2001,Mladenovic1997}.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:igvnd_principle}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The method searches over position assignments and aircraft priority
orders, while a deterministic decoder generates the corresponding
schedule, a separation common in the scheduling literature the method
draws on.  A solution is represented by a \emph{combinatorial state}
$(\pi, \sigma)$, where $\pi : \calR \to \calP$ is the position assignment
and $\sigma$ is a global priority order over the aircraft.  The
\emph{decoder} maps a state to job start and finish times, classifies
every blocking access into its mode (\S\ref{sec:problem}), and returns
the objective value~\eqref{eq:milpobj}, written $z(\pi,\sigma)$.  The
manoeuvre-aware decoder also accepts a per-aircraft timing adjustment,
the \emph{shift} $\theta_r = (d_r, g_r)$, comprising a delay $d_r \ge 0$
of the earliest start and an idle time $g_r \ge 0$ before the last job;
the shift is zero by default and is searched only by the alignment
refinement of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}.  We write $z_1(\pi,\sigma,\theta)$
when the shift matters and $z_1(\pi,\sigma)$ for the zero shift.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:igvnd} applies multiple restarts until the time
budget is exhausted.  Each restart first optimises a zero-movement
schedule and then searches for an improving manoeuvre-aware schedule,
using the decoder of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_decoder}, the construction of
\S\ref{sec:igvnd_construction}, the local search of
\S\ref{sec:igvnd_vnd}, the Iterated Greedy loop of
\S\ref{sec:igvnd_ig} and the alignment refinement of
\S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}; a further zero-movement candidate is built
once at the end (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_safety}).  A restart builds a seed
order (deterministic for the first two, biased-randomised afterwards),
optimises it in the zero-movement regime to obtain a feasible
\emph{zero-movement baseline}, and then re-optimises it in the
manoeuvre-aware regime, where the state also carries the shift $\theta$
started from zero; the two phases split the restart's time slice evenly,
and the manoeuvre-aware schedule replaces the baseline only if it passes
an independent feasibility check and improves it.  A nest-stretch candidate
is added when the weights reward it (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_safety}), and the
best schedule over all restarts is returned.  Because a restart ends
once its own search stalls, small instances perform many independent
restarts within the budget, while larger ones run two long trajectories,
one per clearance geometry of the zero-movement decoder.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\KwData{instance $I$; weights $(W^M, W^D, W^S)$; time budget $T$.}
\KwResult{a feasible schedule.}
preprocess $I$; let $\rho_{\mathrm{NEH}}, \rho_{\mathrm{SLACK}}$ be the two
   deterministic priority rules (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_construction})\;
set the per-restart time cap $\tau = T/8$ if $R \le 10$, $\;T/2$
   otherwise\;
$z^\star \leftarrow +\infty$;\quad $i \leftarrow 1$\;
\While{time remains}{
  allot $\min(\tau,\ \text{remaining time})$ to restart $i$\;
  $\rho \leftarrow \rho_{\mathrm{NEH}}$ if $i{=}1$;\;\quad
     $\rho_{\mathrm{SLACK}}$ if $i{=}2$;\;\quad
     $\textsc{BiasedShuffle}$ of the better-scoring of the two if $i \ge 3$
     \tcp*{Alg.~\ref{alg:construct}}
  $(\pi, \sigma) \leftarrow \textsc{Construct}(\rho)$\;
  $(\pi, \sigma) \leftarrow \textsc{Search}(\pi, \sigma, \textsc{Decode}_0)$;
     \quad $z_{\mathrm{base}} \leftarrow z_0(\pi, \sigma)$
     \tcp*{local-search descent + IG destroy/rebuild (Alg.~\ref{alg:search}); the zero-movement baseline}
  $(\pi', \sigma', \theta') \leftarrow \textsc{Search}(\pi, \sigma, \textsc{Decode}_1)$
     \tcp*{the same search under $\textsc{Decode}_1$ from the zero shift, also aligning $\theta$ (Alg.~\ref{alg:polish})}
  \eIf{$z_1(\pi', \sigma', \theta') < z_{\mathrm{base}}$ \textbf{\emph{and}}
        $\textsc{Decode}_1(\pi', \sigma', \theta')$ passes the feasibility check}{
    $(\sigma^\circ, z^\circ) \leftarrow$ the manoeuvre-aware schedule and its value\;
  }{
    $(\sigma^\circ, z^\circ) \leftarrow$ the baseline schedule and $z_{\mathrm{base}}$\;
  }
  \lIf{$z^\circ < z^\star$}{$z^\star \leftarrow z^\circ$; record the schedule}
  $i \leftarrow i + 1$\;
}
\If{$W^S$ dominates the weights \textbf{\emph{and}} $\calA \ne \emptyset$}{
  build the nest-stretch candidate (\S\ref{sec:igvnd_safety})\;
  adopt it if it is feasible and improves $z^\star$\;
}
\Return the recorded schedule attaining $z^\star$\;
\caption{\textsc{IGVND} --- master procedure.}
\label{alg:igvnd}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Schedule decoder}
\label{sec:igvnd_decoder}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Zero-movement regime.}
This regime produces schedules with no manoeuvres ($n = 0$) that are
feasible by construction: for every blocking arc, both access instants
of the rear aircraft are kept in Mode~A, which leaves three admissible
placements of a rear aircraft's stay relative to a front aircraft,
entirely before it, entirely after it, or enclosing it (the rear enters
before the front starts and leaves after it finishes).  Nesting allows
an aircraft with a longer maintenance stay to enclose the stay of
another, so that two blocking-related aircraft overlap in time instead
of being serialised.  The clearance between an access and the front stay
alternates with the parity of the restart: even restarts allow an access
to coincide with a stay endpoint, as in \S\ref{subsec:access}, and odd
restarts keep a margin of $\eta$.  Aircraft sharing a position are
separated by $\varepsilon$; aircraft on non-conflicting positions run in
parallel.  Processing the aircraft in the priority order $\sigma$, the
decoder places each one at the earliest start outside the intervals
forbidden by the already-placed aircraft (Algorithm~\ref{alg:decode0})
and packs its jobs tight, so $\kappa_j = 0$ for every job.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\KwData{state $(\pi, \sigma)$.}
\KwResult{a feasible schedule with $n = 0$, and its value $z_0$.}
$\mathcal{Q} \leftarrow \emptyset$ \tcp*{aircraft already placed}
\ForEach{$r \in \calR$ \emph{in the order} $\sigma$}{
  $\mathcal{F} \leftarrow \emptyset$ \tcp*{forbidden start instants for $r$}
  \ForEach{$r' \in \mathcal{Q}$}{
    \lIf{$\pi(r') = \pi(r)$}{add to $\mathcal{F}$ the band that violates the
         $\varepsilon$ separation from $r'$}
    \ForEach{$(p, p') \in \calA$ \emph{with} $\{\pi(r), \pi(r')\} = \{p, p'\}$}{
      add to $\mathcal{F}$ the bands that would place an access of the rear
         aircraft strictly inside the front stay (within $\eta$ of it on
         odd restarts)\;
      \tcp*[l]{the admissible residue is: before, after, or enclosing}
    }
  }
  $s_r \leftarrow \min\{\, t \ge E_r : t \notin \mathcal{F} \,\}$\;
  lay the jobs of $r$ back-to-back from $s_r$ with $\kappa_j = 0$\;
  $\mathcal{Q} \leftarrow \mathcal{Q} \cup \{r\}$\;
}
\Return the schedule and $z_0 = W^M m + W^D \sum_r v^D_r$ \tcp*{$n = 0$}
\caption{$\textsc{Decode}_0$ --- zero-movement decoder.}
\label{alg:decode0}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Manoeuvre-aware regime.}
Allowing manoeuvres may reduce makespan or delay when the weights make
them worth their cost.  The positions are processed
deepest-rear first, so that when a front aircraft is laid out the access
instants of all the rear aircraft it blocks are already fixed.  A front
aircraft's start is then chosen to minimise its \emph{local} contribution
to the objective over a candidate set that always contains the
zero-movement placements as well as starts that align a job interior over
an access (allowing a Mode~C access) or a job end on an access (allowing
a Mode~B access); the local cost of a start is evaluated by a forward sweep
that classifies every exposed access and costs the resulting manoeuvres
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:decode1}).  The sweep applies the closed boundary
semantics of the problem statement exactly as the checker does: an access
that coincides with the front's start or finish is Mode~A (the front is
just entering or leaving its slot), an access that coincides with a job
end is Mode~B through the gap that follows that job, and a gap is opened
after the job whose end is \emph{nearest} to the access --- never after an
earlier one, which would waste idle time and extend the stay.  Only
accesses strictly inside a job's $\eta$-margin, or inside a
non-interruptible job, are rejected.  Because the zero-movement
placements remain candidates, this regime can only ever \emph{add} the
manoeuvre option --- it never discards a feasible no-movement schedule.

Processing rears before fronts has a limitation: a rear aircraft is laid
at its earliest feasible start and cannot react to where its front ends
up.  The certified optima of \S\ref{sec:experiments}, however,
frequently delay a rear aircraft, or stretch its stay with idle before its
last job, precisely so that its exit (or entry) coincides with a boundary
of the front aircraft that blocks its access --- the front's stay start or finish, or a job end
--- where the access is Mode~A or Mode~B instead of a Mode~C
interruption.  The decoder therefore honours the per-aircraft shift
$\theta_r = (d_r, g_r)$ introduced in \S\ref{sec:igvnd_principle}: the
lower bound on $r$'s start becomes $E_r + d_r$, and an idle of $g_r$ is
inserted before its last job, so that the finish $f_r$ moves by $g_r$
while the earlier jobs stay where they were.  With $\theta = 0$ the
decoder is exactly the one just described, so the baseline is unaffected;
how $\theta$ is searched is the subject of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}.

The cost of one manoeuvre-aware decode is polynomial.  Let
$A \le 2(|\calR| - 1)$ bound the exposed access instants a front aircraft
faces and $N_{\max}$ the longest job chain: each front evaluates
$O(A\,N_{\max})$ candidate starts, and evaluating one start is an
$O(N_{\max} A^2)$ forward sweep (the $\kappa$ fixpoint scans the accesses
once per widening step).  A full $\textsc{Decode}_1$ call over the fleet is
therefore $O(|\calR|^4 N_{\max}^2)$ in the worst case; in practice $A$
counts only the aircraft assigned to the rear endpoints of blocking arcs,
so the effective cost is far below this bound.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\SetKwProg{Fn}{Procedure}{}{}
\SetKwFunction{FPlace}{PlaceFront}
\KwData{state $(\pi, \sigma)$; shift $\theta = (d_r, g_r)_{r \in \calR}$, zero by default.}
\KwResult{a feasible schedule, possibly with manoeuvres, and its value $z_1$.}
\ForEach{position $p \in \calP$ \emph{in order of decreasing blocking depth}}{
  $\phi \leftarrow -\infty$ \tcp*{finish of the previous aircraft at $p$}
  \ForEach{$r$ \emph{assigned to} $p$, \emph{in the order} $\sigma$}{
    $\ell \leftarrow \max\{E_r + d_r,\ \phi + \varepsilon\}$
       \tcp*{the shift delays the earliest start}
    $A \leftarrow$ the access instants of every already-placed rear of $r$\;
    $(s_r, f_r) \leftarrow \FPlace{$r, \ell, A$}$;\quad $\phi \leftarrow f_r$\;
  }
}
\Return the schedule and its value $z_1$\;
\BlankLine
\Fn{\FPlace{$r$, $\ell$, $A$}}{
  $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \{\ell\}
     \cup \{\, \tau - T_r,\ \tau - \eta - T_r,\ \tau,\ \tau - \eta,\ \tau + \eta : \tau \in A \,\}$
     \tcp*{before / after / nest (touching allowed)}
  $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \mathcal{S}
     \cup \{\text{starts aligning a job interior over some } \tau \in A\}$
     \tcp*{allow Mode C}
  $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \mathcal{S}
     \cup \{\text{starts aligning a job end on some } \tau \in A\}$
     \tcp*{allow Mode B}
  $c^\star \leftarrow +\infty$\;
  \ForEach{$s \in \mathcal{S}$ \emph{with} $s \ge \ell$}{
    sweep the jobs of $r$ from $s$, with an idle of $g_r$ before the last
       job, classifying each $\tau \in A$ as
       Mode~A ($\tau$ outside the stay or exactly on its edges), Mode~B
       (open a gap of width $\ge \mu$ after the job whose end is nearest
       to $\tau$; $\tau$ may sit exactly on that end) or Mode~C (extend
       the job by $\delta$); reject $s$ if any $\tau$ cannot be
       classified or falls strictly inside a job's $\eta$-margin\;
    \If{$s$ \emph{is feasible}}{
      $c \leftarrow W^M f_r + W^D \max\{0, f_r - L_r\} + W^S \cdot 2\nu_s$
         \tcp*{$\nu_s$ = manoeuvres at $s$}
      \lIf{$c < c^\star$}{$c^\star \leftarrow c$; keep $s$ as the best start}
    }
  }
  \Return the best start and its finish\;
}
\caption{$\textsc{Decode}_1$ --- manoeuvre-aware decoder.}
\label{alg:decode1}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Construction}
\label{sec:igvnd_construction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each restart builds its seed by greedy insertion under a priority order
$\rho$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:construct}), the cost of a tentative
insertion being the value of the zero-movement decoder restricted to the
aircraft committed so far.  Two deterministic orders target the makespan
and the delay terms: decreasing total processing time $T_r$ (the NEH
rule) and increasing slack $L_r - E_r - T_r$.  Every restart after the
second draws its order from a rank-biased geometric shuffle of whichever
deterministic order scored better, in which the next aircraft is drawn
from the remaining base order with $\Pr(\text{rank } k) \propto
(1-\beta)^k$; the bias $\beta = 0.3$ was fixed a priori and not tuned,
and a sensitivity sweep over $\beta$ is left to future work.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\KwData{a priority order $\rho$; for the shuffle, a base order and the RNG.}
\KwResult{a seed state $(\pi, \sigma)$.}
\textbf{Greedy insertion under $\rho$:}\;
\Indp
  $\pi \leftarrow \emptyset$\;
  \ForEach{$r \in \calR$ \emph{in the order} $\rho$}{
    $\pi(r) \leftarrow \argmin_{p \in \calP}
       z_0\big(\pi \cup \{r \mapsto p\}\big)$
       \tcp*{cheapest position given the committed prefix}
  }
  \Return $(\pi,\ \rho)$\;
\Indm
\BlankLine
\textbf{\textsc{BiasedShuffle} of a base order $\rho_{\mathrm{base}}$:}\;
\Indp
  $\mathit{pool} \leftarrow \rho_{\mathrm{base}}$;\quad $\rho \leftarrow ()$\;
  \While{$\mathit{pool}$ is not empty}{
    draw a rank $k$ with $\Pr(k) \propto (1-\beta)^{k}$
       \tcp*{$\beta = 0.3$: most mass on ranks 0--3}
    move the $\min(k, |\mathit{pool}|-1)$-th element of $\mathit{pool}$
       to the end of $\rho$\;
  }
  \Return $\rho$ \tcp*{then construct greedily under $\rho$}
\Indm
\caption{\textsc{Construct} --- greedy insertion and the biased shuffle.}
\label{alg:construct}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Local search}
\label{sec:igvnd_vnd}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A seed state is brought to local optimality by a first-improvement
descent, the \textsc{LocalSearch} step invoked within the main loop of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:search}, which explores three neighbourhoods on the
combinatorial state, tried in order for each
aircraft: position reassignment ($N_1$), relocation in the priority
order ($N_2$) and position swap ($N_3$).  Candidates are scanned in
random order, the first strictly improving move is applied, and the
descent terminates when no move yields a strict improvement.  Two
lossless candidate filters keep the cost low.  Under $\textsc{Decode}_0$
the moves are restricted to the aircraft of the \emph{pusher tree}, the
aircraft that, directly or through a chain of pushed starts, delay the
makespan or a late aircraft, since a move involving only other aircraft
cannot improve the objective.  Under both decoders, $N_2$ tests only one
insertion slot per class of slots that yield the same decode, namely
those that leave the aircraft's order relative to the aircraft it
interacts with unchanged.  Under $\textsc{Decode}_1$ the state also
carries the shift $\theta$, which the descent evaluates but never
changes; the shift is searched only by the alignment refinement of
\S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}, because in preliminary experiments searching it
as a fourth neighbourhood multiplied the cost of a non-improving pass
about fivefold and left too little time for the outer loop.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Iterated Greedy loop}
\label{sec:igvnd_ig}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Around the local search runs an Iterated-Greedy loop
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:search}).  Each iteration removes $k$ aircraft,
chosen with equal probability uniformly at random or by a delay-weighted
contribution score, reinserts each at the position and order slot that
minimises the decoded objective, and re-optimises the result with the
local search; the candidate replaces the current state when its
objective value does not exceed the current one, and the best state is
tracked separately.  A removed aircraft loses its shift, which was
aligned to a context the rebuild changes.  The same procedure runs with
$\textsc{Decode}_0$ for the baseline and with $\textsc{Decode}_1$ for
the manoeuvre-aware phase; under $\textsc{Decode}_1$ it calls the
alignment refinement of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_align} at the three points
marked in Algorithm~\ref{alg:search}, the first of them before the
acceptance test because a candidate evaluated with inherited shifts may
overestimate its objective value and be rejected although its assignment
and order are those of a better solution.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\SetKwProg{Fn}{Procedure}{}{}
\SetKwFunction{FPert}{Perturb}
\KwData{seed $(\pi, \sigma)$; decoder $\textsc{Decode}_d$; deadline.}
\KwResult{the best state found.}
$(\pi, \sigma) \leftarrow \textsc{LocalSearch}(\pi, \sigma, \textsc{Decode}_d)$;\quad
   $\theta \leftarrow 0$\;
\lIf{$d = 1$}{$\theta \leftarrow \textsc{Align}(\pi, \sigma, \theta;\ 3)$
   \tcp*[f]{full alignment descent, Alg.~\ref{alg:polish}}}
\emph{best} $\leftarrow$ \emph{cur} $\leftarrow (\pi, \sigma, \theta)$;\quad
   $t \leftarrow 0$ \tcp*{$t$ = non-improving streak}
\Repeat{the deadline is reached \emph{or} $t \ge t_{\max}$}{
  \emph{cand} $\leftarrow \textsc{LocalSearch}\big(\FPert{\emph{cur}},
     \textsc{Decode}_d\big)$
     \tcp*{\FPert{} clears the shift of the aircraft it removes}
  \lIf{$d = 1$}{align \emph{cand} with the single-aircraft step only,
     $\textsc{Align}(\cdot;\ 1)$ \tcp*[f]{judge it with its shifts aligned}}
  \lIf{$d = 1$ \textbf{\emph{and}} $z_d(\emph{cand}) < z_d(\emph{best})$}{align
     \emph{cand} fully, $\textsc{Align}(\cdot;\ 3)$}
  \lIf{$z_d(\emph{cand}) \le z_d(\emph{cur})$}{\emph{cur} $\leftarrow$ \emph{cand}}
  \eIf{$z_d(\emph{cand}) < z_d(\emph{best})$}{
    \emph{best} $\leftarrow$ \emph{cand};\quad $t \leftarrow 0$\;
  }{
        $t \leftarrow t + 1$\;
  }
  \If{$d = 1$ \textbf{\emph{and}} $t \ge t_{\max}$ \textbf{\emph{and}} time remains}{
    \emph{best}$' \leftarrow \textsc{Refine}(\emph{best})$
       \tcp*{shift perturbations, once the search has stalled}
    \lIf{$z_1(\emph{best}') < z_1(\emph{best})$}{\emph{best} $\leftarrow$
       \emph{cur} $\leftarrow$ \emph{best}$'$;\quad $t \leftarrow 0$}
  }
}
\Return \emph{best}\;
\BlankLine
\Fn{\FPert{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$}}{
  with probability $\tfrac12$: remove $k$ aircraft uniformly at random;\;
  otherwise: remove the $k$ aircraft of largest score
     $W^D v^D_r + \omega\, T_r$ (lightly randomised)\;
  \ForEach{removed aircraft $r$}{
        $\theta_r \leftarrow (0, 0)$;\;
        reinsert $r$ at the $(\text{position}, \text{slot in } \sigma)$
       minimising the decoded objective, one slot per distinct-decode class\;
  }
  \Return the rebuilt state\;
}
\caption{\textsc{Search} --- Iterated-Greedy loop around the local search.}
\label{alg:search}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Alignment refinement}
\label{sec:igvnd_align}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The alignment search adjusts $\theta$ so that accesses of an aircraft
coincide with temporal boundaries of the aircraft it interacts with in
the current decoded schedule.  A shift is only ever proposed to put an
access \emph{exactly on such a boundary}, so the candidate set is small,
and the search runs as a refinement of selected states rather than as a
neighbourhood of the descent.

\paragraph{Proposals.}
For an aircraft $r$ on a blocking position, consider the front aircraft
it interacts with in the current schedule: on every position that blocks
$\pi(r)$, those whose stay overlaps $r$'s, plus the last one finishing
before $r$ and the first one starting after it.  Their \emph{boundaries}
are their stay start and finish, their job starts and ends, and the job
completion times they would have if their jobs ran back-to-back from
their start, without inter-job idle time or interruption extensions.  The proposals of $r$ are the shifts that place $r$'s exit (by the
stretch $g_r$ or by the delay $d_r$) or $r$'s entry (by $d_r$) exactly on
one of these boundaries, plus the reset $(0,0)$.  Symmetrically, when $r$
is itself a front, the entry and exit of the rear aircraft on the
positions it blocks are added: a front that stays until its rear leaves,
with the two boundaries coinciding, is how the optima nest several
aircraft along a chain without manoeuvres.

\paragraph{Alignment descent.}
$\textsc{Align}$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:polish}) is a first-improvement
descent over the aircraft on blocking positions, visited in random order,
that uses proposals only.  It considers three types of move, in
increasing order of computational cost: (a) a single aligned shift of an
aircraft $r$; (b) a second aligned shift of $r$ applied after a first
one, since once $r$ moves the decoder re-places its fronts and the
boundary $r$ must land on often exists only after the first move; and
(c) a first aligned shift of $r$ combined with one of the six nearest
aligned shifts of a \emph{related} aircraft $q$ (a front $r$ interacts
with, a rear behind $r$, or a sibling sharing a front with $r$), because
along a chain the shifts cascade and siblings enclosing the same front
move together in the optima.

Moves of types (b) and (c) are restricted to the twelve first shifts
nearest to $r$'s current shift, and type (b) to the six second shifts
nearest to each.  Type (b) is evaluated only after no aircraft improves
under type (a), type (c) only after none improves under type (b), and
after any improvement the descent returns to type (a); the end state is
a local optimum for all three types.  A level argument stops the
escalation early: $\textsc{Align}(\cdot;\ 1)$ evaluates type (a) alone,
the form applied to every Iterated-Greedy candidate.

\paragraph{Shift perturbations.}
The descent only reaches boundaries of the \emph{current} schedule,
whereas some improvements require coordinated shifts of several aircraft
to instants that become boundaries only after the others have moved, and
cannot be reached through single-aircraft improving moves.
$\textsc{Refine}$ therefore follows the descent with an iterated local
search on the shift alone: it perturbs one to three random aircraft,
each to one of its own proposals drawn at random (not necessarily
improving), re-runs the alignment descent, and keeps the result if it is
better; it stops after as many consecutive non-improving perturbations
as there are candidate aircraft, or when its budget is spent.  A
refinement may use at most a quarter of the time left in the phase, so
that alignment on a large hub does not consume the search itself.
Within Algorithm~\ref{alg:search} the perturbations run only once the
search has stalled, so that they do not compete for time with an outer
loop that is still improving.  The mechanism adds one component to the
state; its candidate caps, perturbation sizes and time budget were fixed
a priori and not tuned.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInput{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInput{KwResult}{Output}
\SetKwProg{Fn}{Procedure}{}{}
\SetKwFunction{FAlign}{Align}
\SetKwFunction{FPolish}{Refine}
\KwData{state $(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$ under $\textsc{Decode}_1$; deepest
   step $L \in \{1, 3\}$; refinement budget.}
\KwResult{an aligned shift $\theta$.}
\Fn{\FAlign{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$; $L$}}{
  $\mathit{level} \leftarrow$ (a)\;
  \While{$\mathit{level} \le L$ \textbf{\emph{and}} time remains}{
    $\mathit{found} \leftarrow$ false\;
    \ForEach{aircraft $r$ on a blocking position, in random order}{
      try the proposals of $r$ at $\mathit{level}$: (a) one alignment of
         $r$; (b) a second alignment of $r$ after a first; (c) a first
         alignment of $r$ plus one of a related aircraft $q$\;
      \lIf{one strictly lowers $z_1(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$}{apply it;
         $\mathit{found} \leftarrow$ true; \textbf{break}}
    }
    $\mathit{level} \leftarrow$ (a) if $\mathit{found}$, else the next level\;
  }
  \Return $\theta$\;
}
\BlankLine
\Fn{\FPolish{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$}}{
  $\theta \leftarrow \FAlign{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$; $3$}$;\quad
     $\mathit{fails} \leftarrow 0$\;
  \While{$\mathit{fails} <$ number of candidate aircraft \textbf{\emph{and}}
         budget remains}{
    $\theta' \leftarrow \theta$ with one to three random aircraft each set
       to one of its own proposals, drawn at random\;
    $\theta' \leftarrow \FAlign{$(\pi, \sigma, \theta')$; $3$}$\;
    \lIf{$z_1(\pi, \sigma, \theta') < z_1(\pi, \sigma, \theta)$}{$\theta
       \leftarrow \theta'$;\quad $\mathit{fails} \leftarrow 0$}
    \lElse{$\mathit{fails} \leftarrow \mathit{fails} + 1$}
  }
  \Return $\theta$\;
}
\caption{\textsc{Align} and \textsc{Refine} --- the alignment refinement
   of the per-aircraft shift.}
\label{alg:polish}
\end{algorithm}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Nest-stretch candidate}
\label{sec:igvnd_safety}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When the movement-priority profile meets a blocking topology,
Algorithm~\ref{alg:igvnd} adds one further zero-movement candidate, the
\emph{nest-stretch} schedule, constructed once and adopted under the
same feasibility-check rule as the restarts: the stays of rear aircraft are
extended with inter-job idle so that they enclose the stays of the
aircraft blocking them.  Zero-movement optima often have this shape,
which the earliest-feasible decode cannot produce because it always
prefers placing an aircraft before a front over enclosing it, and packs
jobs tight.  The candidate is written with explicit start times.
Aircraft are grouped into rounds of one per position; within a round,
along every front$\to$rear arc the rear's stay is extended to enclose the
front's stay with a margin of $\eta$ on both sides, so that the deepest
rear has the longest stay, while positions that block no other run tight
and in parallel.  Rounds are serialised within the blocking component
and chained per position elsewhere; four round partitions (long-first,
short-first, earliest-release, round-robin) are tried and the best is
kept.
