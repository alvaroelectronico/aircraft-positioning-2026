\section{Computational Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
% ==============================================================================

This section compares the IGVND heuristic of \S\ref{sec:heuristic} against
the exact MILP of \S\ref{sec:milp} on a controlled benchmark.  The MILP is
solved with Gurobi and serves as the reference: where it converges it
provides a proven optimum; where it does not, its 60\,s incumbent is the
best exact-solver result obtainable under the same budget.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Benchmark design}
\label{sec:benchmark}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The benchmark isolates four axes of difficulty: the blocking topology, the
fleet size $R$, the time-window slack, and a hard dense-plus-large
configuration.  All instances use $P = 5$ positions.  Six topology families
span the spectrum from independent to maximally constrained hangars:
\emph{None} ($\calA = \emptyset$), \emph{Chain}
($\calA = \{(p_i, p_{i+1})\}$), \emph{Hub} (one aisle position blocks all
others), \emph{Triangle} (a transitive triangle on the rear trio, the
layout of the facility that motivated this study), \emph{Two rows} (front
and rear halves paired position-by-position), and \emph{Full} (every
ordered pair is a blocking arc).  The benchmark comprises 12 configurations,
each replicated with 10 independently seeded instances, for
\textbf{120 instances} in total (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}).  Processing
times, earliest starts and due dates are sampled on an integer-day grid;
the tow time is fixed at $\varepsilon = 0.5$ days.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both methods are run on every instance under three weight profiles that
each isolate one objective term: makespan-priority
$w^{\mathrm{MK}} = (100, 1, 1)$, delay-priority
$w^{\mathrm{DLY}} = (1, 100, 1)$, and movement-priority
$w^{\mathrm{MOV}} = (1, 1, 100)$, for $(W^M, W^D, W^S)$.  Each run is given
a strictly enforced wall-clock budget of 60\,s.  We report the relative
objective gap
\[
  g = \frac{z_{\mathrm{MILP}} - z_{\mathrm{IGVND}}}{z_{\mathrm{MILP}}},
\]
so that $g > 0$ means IGVND attains the lower (better) objective on this
minimisation problem.  Because the relative gap is distorted by small
denominators when the optimal delay is near zero, we also report the
undistorted per-component differences (IGVND $-$ MILP) for makespan, delay
and movement count.  Both quantities are aggregated as the mean over the
10 seeds of each configuration.  All IGVND solutions are validated by the
independent feasibility checker before being scored; across the full
$120 \times 3 = 360$ heuristic runs there were no feasibility failures.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{MILP convergence}
\label{sec:milp_conv}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:milp_conv} records how often the MILP proves optimality
within the budget.  The pattern frames the entire comparison.  The MILP is
reliable as a \emph{proven optimum} only on the small and loosely
constrained cases: the five-aircraft instances and the no-blocking baseline
solve to optimality in well under a second on all profiles, and the
loosely-constrained triangle instances solve on every seed.  As soon as the
fleet grows ($R \ge 20$), the topology becomes dense (\emph{Full}), or the
profile activates the manoeuvre machinery (\emph{delay-} and
\emph{movement-priority} couple the timing and classification binaries far
more tightly than makespan-priority), the MILP no longer converges in
60\,s and returns a feasible incumbent with a non-zero optimality gap.
On those configurations a positive $g$ does not mean IGVND beats a proven
optimum; it means IGVND finds a better feasible solution than the exact
solver does in the same time.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:gap_profile} reports the relative gap and
Table~\ref{tab:components} the per-component differences.  We read them
together, grouping the configurations by what the MILP can certify.

\paragraph{Where the MILP is a proven optimum, IGVND matches it.}
On the configurations the MILP solves to optimality, IGVND is at or within
a small fraction of a percent of that optimum.  It is \emph{exact} on the
no-blocking baseline and on the five-aircraft instances (mean
$|g| \le 0.2\%$ across all profiles), and within roughly $\pm 2.5\%$ on the
remaining converged $R{=}10$ cases.  The makespan-priority column, where
the MILP converges on most $R{=}10$ topologies, shows IGVND tracking the
optimum closely (\emph{None}, \emph{Triangle}, \emph{Two rows} all within
$1\%$); the small negative entries on \emph{Chain} and \emph{Hub} are at
the level of the search's run-to-run noise.  The corresponding
$\Delta m, \Delta D, \Delta n$ in Table~\ref{tab:components} are
near-zero, confirming that these are genuine near-ties rather than
denominator artefacts.

\paragraph{The one apparent outlier is a small-denominator artefact.}
The most negative entry in Table~\ref{tab:gap_profile} is the
loosely-constrained triangle under delay-priority ($-19\%$ mean, driven by
a single seed at $-199\%$).  Here the MILP drives the total delay to zero
by spending a handful of manoeuvres, so the denominator $z_{\mathrm{MILP}}$
is tiny and any residual delay explodes the relative gap.  The
corresponding $\Delta D$ in Table~\ref{tab:components} is essentially zero:
in absolute terms IGVND loses a negligible amount of delay, not a large
one.  This is exactly the small-denominator inflation the per-component
table is meant to expose.

\paragraph{Where the MILP times out, IGVND wins decisively.}
On the configurations the MILP cannot close in 60\,s --- every
$R{=}20$ and $R{=}30$ case, the dense \emph{Full} topology, and the
delay-/movement-priority profiles on the tight $R{=}10$ topologies ---
IGVND returns a substantially better feasible solution within the same
budget.  The gap reaches $+17\%$ to $+41\%$ on the medium and large
triangles, $+56\%$ on the hard dense-plus-large \emph{Full}-$R{=}20$
configuration under delay-priority, and the per-component differences
($\Delta D$ of several hundred to over a thousand delay units, $\Delta m$
of tens to over a hundred makespan units, both negative) show this is a
large absolute improvement and not a denominator effect.  The mechanism is
the architecture of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_principle}: by searching over the
compact combinatorial state and pricing it with a fast decoder, IGVND
evaluates orders of magnitude more candidate schedules per second than the
branch-and-bound search can while it is still struggling to tighten its
bound.

\paragraph{Movement-priority and dense blocking.}
The movement-priority profile is the hardest for a heuristic, because both
methods typically reach a zero-manoeuvre schedule and the winner is then
simply the one that packs the schedule tightest.  The dense
concentric-nesting builder of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_safety} is what makes IGVND
competitive here: on the dense \emph{Full}-$R{=}10$ topology it lets the
heuristic build the two-wave nesting that the earliest-feasible decode
cannot, and IGVND additionally wins outright on \emph{Chain} ($-10.4\%$)
and \emph{Hub} ($-4.0\%$).  The few configurations where IGVND still trails
under movement-priority are precisely those where the MILP itself has not
converged, so there is no reliable optimum to chase.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The two methods are complementary rather than competing.  The MILP is the
instrument of record on instances it can close: it certifies optimality on
the small and loosely constrained cases, and it is the yardstick that lets
us \emph{measure} the heuristic's quality there --- where IGVND is shown to
be exact or near-exact.  IGVND is the instrument of choice at operational
scale: on every configuration the MILP cannot close within the budget, the
heuristic delivers a markedly better feasible schedule in the same 60\,s,
with a guaranteed-feasible zero-movement floor and a checker-validated
manoeuvre-aware polish.  Read jointly, the MILP validates the heuristic on
the tractable end of the benchmark and the heuristic extends usable
solution quality to the intractable end --- which is the regime an
operator actually faces.
