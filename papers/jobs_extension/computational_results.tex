% ==============================================================================
\section{Computational Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
% ==============================================================================

This section compares the IGVND heuristic of \S\ref{sec:heuristic} against
the exact MILP of \S\ref{sec:milp} on a controlled benchmark.  The MILP is
solved with Gurobi and serves as the reference: where it converges it
provides a proven optimum; where it does not, its 60\,s incumbent is the
best exact-solver result obtainable under the same budget.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Benchmark design}
\label{sec:benchmark}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The benchmark isolates three axes of difficulty: the blocking topology, the
fleet size $R$, and the time-window slack.  All instances use $P = 5$
positions.  Four topology families span the spectrum from independent to
constrained hangars: \emph{None} ($\calA = \emptyset$), \emph{Chain}
($\calA = \{(p_i, p_{i+1})\}$, the nose-to-tail single-lane hangar),
\emph{Hub} (one aisle position blocks all others), and \emph{Two rows}
(front and rear halves paired position-by-position, the common
drive-through layout).  \emph{None} is evaluated at the baseline
($R = 10$, tight windows) as the no-blocking control.  Each of the three
blocking topologies is crossed over four fleet sizes
($R \in \{5, 10, 20, 30\}$) and three slack levels (loose, medium,
tight), so the effects of blocking structure, size and time-window
tightness can each be read independently.  This gives
\textbf{37 configurations}, each replicated with 10 independently seeded
instances, for \textbf{370 instances} in total (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}).
Processing times, earliest starts and due dates are sampled on an integer-day
grid; the tow time is fixed at $\varepsilon = 0.5$ days.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both methods are run on every instance under three weight profiles that
each isolate one objective term: makespan-priority
$w^{\mathrm{MK}} = (100, 1, 1)$, delay-priority
$w^{\mathrm{DLY}} = (1, 100, 1)$, and movement-priority
$w^{\mathrm{MOV}} = (1, 1, 100)$, for $(W^M, W^D, W^S)$.  Each run is given
a strictly enforced wall-clock budget of 60\,s.  We report the relative
objective gap
\[
  g = \frac{z_{\mathrm{MILP}} - z_{\mathrm{IGVND}}}{z_{\mathrm{MILP}}},
\]
so that $g > 0$ means IGVND attains the lower (better) objective on this
minimisation problem.  Because the relative gap is distorted by small
denominators when the optimal delay is near zero, we also report the
undistorted per-component differences (IGVND $-$ MILP) for makespan, delay
and movement count.  Both quantities are aggregated as the mean over the
10 seeds of each configuration.  All IGVND solutions are validated by the
independent feasibility checker before being scored; across the full
$370 \times 3 = 1110$ heuristic runs there were no feasibility failures.
IGVND is implemented in Python; its two decoders, the neighbourhood scan,
the reinsertion step and the alignment step are compiled with Numba, and
the pure-Python fallback produces identical search trajectories, so the
compilation changes throughput but not behaviour.  The heuristic runs
reported here were executed three at a time on a laptop (Intel
i7-11390H, 4 cores, 16\,GB RAM, Windows~11, Python~3.14, Numba~0.68);
every run was validated by the checker as it finished.

\subsection{MILP convergence}
\label{sec:milp_conv}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:milp_conv} reports the mean optimality gap Gurobi still
reports at the end of the budget, per configuration and profile.  The pattern
frames the entire comparison.  The MILP is reliable as a \emph{proven optimum}
(mean gap $0.0\%$) only on the small and loosely constrained cases: every
five-aircraft configuration and the no-blocking baseline close on all
profiles in well under a second, and the loose $R{=}10$ configurations
mostly follow (\emph{Two rows} on every profile; \emph{Hub} and
\emph{Chain} except for residual gaps on isolated profiles, worst
$22\%$ on \emph{Chain} under movement-priority).  As
soon as the fleet grows ($R \ge 20$) or the profile activates the manoeuvre
machinery (\emph{delay-} and \emph{movement-priority} couple the timing and
classification binaries far more tightly than makespan-priority), the gap blows
up: it exceeds $63\%$ at $R{=}20$, climbs to around $74$--$81\%$ at $R{=}30$, and reaches
essentially $100\%$ under the delay-priority profile, where Gurobi returns a
feasible incumbent without closing the bound at all.  Makespan-priority is
consistently the easiest profile for the exact solver (smallest gaps),
delay-priority the hardest.

Even where it cannot close the bound, the exact solver returned a feasible
incumbent within the budget on every run of the benchmark.  On every
configuration with a non-zero gap, a positive $g$ in
Table~\ref{tab:gap_profile} therefore does not mean IGVND beats a proven
optimum; it means IGVND finds a better feasible solution than the exact
solver does in the same time.

\paragraph{Decoder expressiveness and reachability.}
Before comparing objectives we verify that the manoeuvre-aware decoder can
\emph{express} the exact solver's schedules, since no amount of search can
reach a structure outside the decoder's image.  For every instance--profile
pair the MILP closes at $R \le 10$ (439 pairs), we feed
$\textsc{Decode}_1$ the MILP's own position assignment and start order
with the zero shift.  All 439 decodes pass the checker, and 163 reproduce
the certified objective exactly.  Reading extra inputs off the MILP
schedule identifies what the remainder need: adding only the per-aircraft
start delays reproduces 176 optima, adding only the inter-job idle gaps
373, and adding both all 439; restricting the inputs to aircraft on rear
positions, with one idle gap before the last job, still reproduces 411.
A minimal-input analysis of the 276 cells the zero shift misses shows
that a single aircraft suffices in 131 of them and at most two in 210,
that over nine in ten of the inputs needed are a stretch of an aircraft
on a rear position, and that in about 95\% of those the stretched exit
coincides exactly with a boundary of an aircraft at a blocking front
position.  This is the structure the shift $\theta$ and the alignment
polish of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_align} were built for, and the same oracle
measures how much of it they recover: started from the MILP's own
assignment and order with the zero shift, the alignment descent alone
reaches 388 of the 439 certified optima and the shift kicks raise it to
413, every decode checker-compliant.  Whether the search also finds the
right assignment and order is what the results below measure.  The
expressiveness check is also what fixed the closed-boundary semantics of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:decode1}: an earlier version of the sweep rejected
accesses lying exactly on a stay edge or a job end, which the problem
statement (and the checker) admit, and opened Mode~B gaps after the
earliest rather than the nearest job end.  Both checks are kept as
regression tests of the implementation.

\paragraph{Instances the MILP solves to optimality.}
Over the 439 instance--profile pairs the MILP closes at $R \le 10$,
IGVND reproduces the certified objective \emph{exactly} in 404.  On the
90 five-aircraft instances it is exact on every seed under
movement-priority (90 of 90), on 89 of 90 under makespan-priority and
on 88 of 90 under delay-priority, and the no-blocking baseline is exact
under all three profiles.  The three five-aircraft concessions are each
a single seed trading one objective term against another: on
\emph{Chain}-tight seed~2 IGVND saves a manoeuvre at the price of four
delay units under makespan-priority ($+2$ objective units) and spends
an extra manoeuvre and one makespan unit under delay-priority ($+3$);
on \emph{Hub}-tight seed~7 it saves a manoeuvre at the price of one
delay unit under delay-priority ($+98$ units on an optimum of $341$,
the $-29\%$ seed behind the $-2.9\%$ cell).  On the $R{=}10$ cells the
MILP closes on every seed, IGVND is exact on every seed of \emph{Two
rows}-loose under delay- and movement-priority, \emph{Two rows}-medium
under makespan-priority and \emph{Hub}-loose under movement-priority,
and on nine of ten under \emph{Two rows}-loose makespan-priority and
\emph{Hub}-loose delay-priority.  The remaining converged cells sit in
the makespan-priority column: on \emph{Chain}-loose and
\emph{Chain}-medium IGVND is exact on five seeds of ten and ends a mean
$0.3$ makespan units above the optimum on the rest ($g = -0.4\%$, worst
seed $-1.6\%$), and on \emph{Hub}-medium a mean $0.9$ units above it
($-1.4\%$, worst seed $-8.0\%$).  On every $R{=}10$ makespan-priority
cell, converged or not, IGVND tracks the reference within $-1.4\%$.

\paragraph{Delay-priority residuals.}
The delay-priority column keeps four negative cells against certified
optima --- the loose $R{=}10$ rows of \emph{Chain} ($-3.8\%$) and
\emph{Hub} ($-6.2\%$) and the tight $R{=}5$ rows of \emph{Hub}
($-2.9\%$) and \emph{Chain} ($-0.8\%$) --- and each is carried by one
to four seeds conceding at most half a delay unit, one makespan unit or
one manoeuvre on an optimum whose delay is near zero; the mean
$\Delta D$ behind every one of them is between $0.0$ and $+0.1$
(Table~\ref{tab:components}).  The single worst seed of the benchmark,
\emph{Hub}-loose $R{=}10$ seed~5, concedes half a delay unit and half a
makespan unit against two saved manoeuvres ($126.5$ against $78$,
$-62\%$), and only three of the 1110 runs end more than $20\%$ above
the reference.  The fifth negative cell, \emph{Hub}-medium at $R{=}10$
($-0.7\%$), is a near-tie with a timed-out incumbent (five seeds won,
three lost).  The denominator effect behind these rows is best seen by
comparison: with the alignment polish of \S\ref{sec:igvnd_align}
disabled, the same two $R{=}10$-loose cells read $-111\%$ and $-148\%$
while conceding a mean of $1.9$ delay units each, and eight runs ended
more than $100\%$ above the reference.  The per-component columns, not
the relative gap, remain the meaningful scale on these rows.

\paragraph{Instances the MILP cannot close within the budget.}
On the configurations the MILP cannot close in 60\,s the comparison
splits by scale.  At $R{=}10$ the timed-out incumbents remain
competitive, but IGVND already beats them wherever the windows are
tight: under delay-priority on \emph{Chain}-tight ($+16.2\%$, all ten
seeds), \emph{Hub}-tight ($+7.5\%$, all ten), \emph{Chain}-medium
($+5.6\%$, nine of ten) and \emph{Two rows}-tight ($+2.0\%$), and
under makespan- and movement-priority on \emph{Chain}-tight ($+2.0\%$
and $+4.4\%$); elsewhere the incumbent edges the heuristic by at most
$1.4\%$.  From $R{=}20$ the picture is uniform: IGVND returns the
better feasible solution on \emph{every} configuration, profile and
seed --- 540 runs without a single loss --- and the advantage grows
with fleet size: relative gaps from $+15.6\%$ to $+39.8\%$ at $R{=}20$
and from $+20.6\%$ to $+52.9\%$ at $R{=}30$, across all three blocking
topologies and all three slack levels, peaking under delay-priority on
\emph{Chain}-loose-$R{=}30$ ($+52.9\%$); \emph{Hub}, whose $R{=}30$
instances the exact solver bounds best, is the mildest family at
$+20.6$--$31.1\%$.  The per-component differences confirm a large
absolute improvement and not a denominator effect: mean $\Delta D$ from
roughly $-110$ to $-290$ delay units at $R{=}20$ and from $-600$ to
$-1\,680$ at $R{=}30$, and $\Delta m$ from $-22$ to $-49$ and from
$-53$ to $-141$ makespan units, negative throughout.  A detail worth
noting on \emph{Chain} at scale: under the makespan- and delay-priority
profiles IGVND spends \emph{more} manoeuvres than the incumbent (mean
$\Delta n$ up to $+47$ movements at $R{=}30$) and still wins by more
than a thousand delay units --- the manoeuvre machinery being used as
intended, buying schedule compression at the single-lane bottleneck.
The mechanism is the architecture of
\S\ref{sec:igvnd_principle}: by searching over the compact combinatorial
state and pricing it with a fast decoder, IGVND evaluates orders of magnitude
more candidate schedules per second than the branch-and-bound search can
while it is still struggling to tighten its bound.

\paragraph{Movement-priority profile.}
The movement-priority profile is the hardest for a heuristic.  Both methods
almost always reach a zero-manoeuvre schedule ($\Delta n = 0$ throughout), so
the heavily-weighted movement term contributes nothing and the objective
collapses to makespan plus delay; the winner is then simply whichever packs
the zero-movement schedule tightest.  Because that denominator is not small
(it ranges from $\approx 36$ at $R{=}5$ to several thousand at $R{=}30$), the
relative gaps here are genuine and not a small-denominator artefact --- unlike
the delay-priority residuals of the previous paragraph.  IGVND matches the MILP
\emph{exactly} on every five-aircraft seed of every topology and slack
(90 of 90, $g = 0.00\%$) and on every seed of the four $R{=}10$ cells
the MILP closes under this profile, concedes at most $0.5\%$ on the
other $R{=}10$ cells (\emph{Chain}-loose, worst seed $-2.5\%$,
per-component concessions within $0.2$ units) while beating the
timed-out incumbent on the tight ones (\emph{Chain} $+4.4\%$, \emph{Hub}
$+2.8\%$), and dominates at $R{=}20$ and $R{=}30$ ($+19\%$ to $+45\%$,
with large negative $\Delta m$ and $\Delta D$ --- hundreds to over a
thousand objective units in absolute terms).
Two ingredients hold the zero-movement packing at or near the certified
optima: a nest-stretch candidate that reproduces the optimum's
stay-stretching mechanism over the actual blocking digraph
(\S\ref{sec:igvnd_safety}), and
the random half of the destruction step, which keeps the walk from anchoring
in one basin.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The two methods are complementary rather than competing.  The MILP is the
instrument of record on instances it can close: it certifies optimality on
the small and loosely constrained cases, and it is the yardstick that lets
us \emph{measure} the heuristic's quality there --- where IGVND is shown to
be exact or near-exact.  At $R{=}10$ its timed-out incumbents remain
competitive on the blocking topologies.  IGVND is the instrument of choice
at operational scale: on every configuration at $R{=}20$ and beyond, the
heuristic delivers a markedly better feasible schedule in the same 60\,s,
with a guaranteed-feasible zero-movement floor and a checker-validated
manoeuvre-aware polish.  Read jointly, the MILP validates the heuristic on
the tractable end of the benchmark and the heuristic extends usable
solution quality to the intractable end --- which is the regime an
operator actually faces.
