\section{Computational Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
% ==============================================================================

This section compares the IGVND heuristic of \S\ref{sec:heuristic} against
the exact MILP of \S\ref{sec:milp} on a controlled benchmark.  The MILP is
solved with Gurobi and serves as the reference: where it converges it
provides a proven optimum; where it does not, its 60\,s incumbent is the
best exact-solver result obtainable under the same budget.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Benchmark design}
\label{sec:benchmark}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The benchmark isolates three axes of difficulty: the blocking topology, the
fleet size $R$, and the time-window slack.  All instances use $P = 5$
positions.  Four topology families span the spectrum from independent to
constrained hangars: \emph{None} ($\calA = \emptyset$), \emph{Chain}
($\calA = \{(p_i, p_{i+1})\}$, the nose-to-tail single-lane hangar),
\emph{Hub} (one aisle position blocks all others), and \emph{Two rows}
(front and rear halves paired position-by-position, the common
drive-through layout).  \emph{None} is evaluated at the baseline
($R = 10$, tight windows) as the no-blocking control.  Each of the three
blocking topologies is crossed over four fleet sizes
($R \in \{5, 10, 20, 30\}$) and three slack levels (loose, medium,
tight), so the effects of blocking structure, size and time-window
tightness can each be read independently.  This gives
\textbf{37 configurations}, each replicated with 10 independently seeded
instances, for \textbf{370 instances} in total (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark}).
Processing times, earliest starts and due dates are sampled on an integer-day
grid; the tow time is fixed at $\varepsilon = 0.5$ days.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both methods are run on every instance under three weight profiles that
each isolate one objective term: makespan-priority
$w^{\mathrm{MK}} = (100, 1, 1)$, delay-priority
$w^{\mathrm{DLY}} = (1, 100, 1)$, and movement-priority
$w^{\mathrm{MOV}} = (1, 1, 100)$, for $(W^M, W^D, W^S)$.  Each run is given
a strictly enforced wall-clock budget of 60\,s.  We report the relative
objective gap
\[
  g = \frac{z_{\mathrm{MILP}} - z_{\mathrm{IGVND}}}{z_{\mathrm{MILP}}},
\]
so that $g > 0$ means IGVND attains the lower (better) objective on this
minimisation problem.  Because the relative gap is distorted by small
denominators when the optimal delay is near zero, we also report the
undistorted per-component differences (IGVND $-$ MILP) for makespan, delay
and movement count.  Both quantities are aggregated as the mean over the
10 seeds of each configuration.  All IGVND solutions are validated by the
independent feasibility checker before being scored; across the full
$370 \times 3 = 1110$ heuristic runs there were no feasibility failures.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{MILP convergence}
\label{sec:milp_conv}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:milp_conv} reports the mean optimality gap Gurobi still
reports at the end of the budget, per configuration and profile.  The pattern
frames the entire comparison.  The MILP is reliable as a \emph{proven optimum}
(mean gap $0.0\%$) only on the small and loosely constrained cases: every
five-aircraft configuration and the no-blocking baseline close on all
profiles in well under a second, and the loose $R{=}10$ configurations
mostly follow (\emph{Two rows} on every profile; \emph{Hub} and
\emph{Chain} except for residual gaps on isolated profiles, worst
$22\%$ on \emph{Chain} under movement-priority).  As
soon as the fleet grows ($R \ge 20$) or the profile activates the manoeuvre
machinery (\emph{delay-} and \emph{movement-priority} couple the timing and
classification binaries far more tightly than makespan-priority), the gap blows
up: it exceeds $63\%$ at $R{=}20$, climbs to around $74$--$81\%$ at $R{=}30$, and reaches
essentially $100\%$ under the delay-priority profile, where Gurobi returns a
feasible incumbent without closing the bound at all.  Makespan-priority is
consistently the easiest profile for the exact solver (smallest gaps),
delay-priority the hardest.

Even where it cannot close the bound, the exact solver returned a feasible
incumbent within the budget on every run of the benchmark.  On every
configuration with a non-zero gap, a positive $g$ in
Table~\ref{tab:gap_profile} therefore does not mean IGVND beats a proven
optimum; it means IGVND finds a better feasible solution than the exact
solver does in the same time.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:gap_profile} reports the relative gap and
Table~\ref{tab:components} the per-component differences.  We read them
together, grouping the configurations by what the MILP can certify.

\paragraph{Decoder expressiveness check.}
Before comparing objectives we verify that the manoeuvre-aware decoder can
\emph{express} the exact solver's schedules, since no amount of search can
reach a structure outside the decoder's image.  For every instance--profile
pair the MILP closes at $R \le 10$ (439 pairs), we feed
$\textsc{Decode}_1$ the MILP's own position assignment and start order.
All 439 decodes pass the checker, and 163 reproduce the certified objective
exactly; the remainder differ because the decoder lays each rear aircraft
at its earliest feasible start, whereas the optimum sometimes delays or
stretches a rear so that its access lands on a boundary of the front it
blocks --- a structure the search reaches through other assignments, as
the results below show.  This check is what fixed the closed-boundary
semantics of Algorithm~\ref{alg:decode1}: an earlier version of the sweep
rejected accesses lying exactly on a stay edge or a job end, which the
problem statement (and the checker) admit, and opened Mode~B gaps after
the earliest rather than the nearest job end.  The check is kept as a
regression test of the implementation.

\paragraph{Instances the MILP solves to optimality.}
On the configurations the MILP solves to optimality, IGVND is exact or
concedes only a few objective units.  On the 90 five-aircraft
instances it matches the certified optimum \emph{exactly} on every seed
under movement-priority (90 of 90) and on 88 of 90 under both
makespan- and delay-priority; the four remaining concessions are one or
two objective units (four delay units traded against a saved manoeuvre)
under makespan-priority and a single delay unit under delay-priority, and
the no-blocking baseline is exact under all three profiles.  On the converged $R{=}10$
cells --- \emph{Two rows}-loose under every profile, \emph{Hub}-loose
under delay- and movement-priority --- the concessions are equally
small in absolute terms (mean $\Delta m \le +0.1$ and
$\Delta D \le +2.2$ in Table~\ref{tab:components}), although the
relative gap column can look large because of the small denominators
discussed next.  The visible certified-optimum concessions sit in the
makespan-priority column at $R{=}10$: on \emph{Chain}-loose and
\emph{Chain}-medium the MILP proves optimality on every seed and IGVND
ends a mean $3.1$--$3.2$ makespan units above it
($g = -5.1\%$ and $-4.9\%$, worst seed $-6.8\%$), with the same
pattern at smaller scale on \emph{Hub}-medium ($-2.5\%$,
$\Delta m = +1.6$).  On the remaining $R{=}10$ makespan-priority cells,
converged or not, IGVND tracks the reference within $-2.5\%$.

\paragraph{Delay-priority outliers.}
The delay-priority column contains two large negative cells --- the
loose $R{=}10$ rows of \emph{Chain} ($-141\%$) and \emph{Hub}
($-138\%$) --- and a smaller one on \emph{Chain}-tight at $R{=}5$
($-25\%$), all artefacts of the gap's denominator, not large physical
differences.  On these
configurations the optimal delay is zero or near zero, so
$z_{\mathrm{MILP}}$ reduces to the lightly weighted makespan and movement
terms, and a difference of a few delay units --- weighted $100$ ---
swamps that baseline: the mean $\Delta D$ behind those cells is
between $+0.1$ and $+1.8$ delay units (Table~\ref{tab:components}),
with single seeds registering relative gaps beyond $-600\%$ while
conceding five delay units, or $-250\%$ for a single one.  The per-component columns, not the
relative gap, are the meaningful scale on these rows.

\paragraph{Instances the MILP cannot close within the budget.}
On the configurations the MILP cannot close in 60\,s the comparison
splits by scale.  At $R{=}10$ the timed-out incumbents remain
competitive: IGVND beats them clearly under delay-priority on the
tight topologies (\emph{Chain} $+10.4\%$, winning all ten seeds;
\emph{Hub} $+5.3\%$), while elsewhere the incumbent edges the heuristic
by a few objective units.  From $R{=}20$ the picture is uniform: IGVND
returns the better
feasible solution on \emph{every} configuration and profile, and the
advantage grows with fleet size --- relative gaps of
roughly $+11\%$ to $+27\%$ at $R{=}20$ and $+12\%$ to $+44\%$ at
$R{=}30$, across all three blocking topologies
and all three slack levels, peaking under delay-priority ($+43.8\%$ on
\emph{Two rows}-loose-$R{=}30$, all ten seeds won; \emph{Hub}, whose
$R{=}30$ instances the exact solver bounds best, is the mildest
family at $+12$--$22\%$).  The per-component
differences confirm a large absolute improvement and not a denominator
effect: mean $\Delta D$ from roughly $-70$ to $-1\,300$ delay units and
$\Delta m$ from tens to over $130$ makespan units, negative throughout.
A detail worth noting on \emph{Chain} at scale: under the makespan- and
delay-priority profiles IGVND spends \emph{more} manoeuvres than the
incumbent (mean $\Delta n$ up to $+73$ movements at $R{=}30$) and still
wins by hundreds of delay units --- the manoeuvre machinery being used
as intended, buying schedule compression at the single-lane
bottleneck.  The mechanism is the architecture of
\S\ref{sec:igvnd_principle}: by searching over the compact combinatorial
state and pricing it with a fast decoder, IGVND evaluates orders of magnitude
more candidate schedules per second than the branch-and-bound search can
while it is still struggling to tighten its bound.

\paragraph{Movement-priority profile.}
The movement-priority profile is the hardest for a heuristic.  Both methods
almost always reach a zero-manoeuvre schedule ($\Delta n = 0$ throughout), so
the heavily-weighted movement term contributes nothing and the objective
collapses to makespan plus delay; the winner is then simply whichever packs
the zero-movement schedule tightest.  Because that denominator is not small
(it ranges from $\approx 36$ at $R{=}5$ to several thousand at $R{=}30$), the
relative gaps here are genuine and not a small-denominator artefact --- unlike
the delay-priority outliers of the previous paragraph.  IGVND matches the MILP
\emph{exactly} on every five-aircraft seed of every topology and slack
(90 of 90, $g = 0.00\%$), stays within $\pm 3\%$ of the reference on
every $R{=}10$ cell (worst $-1.0\%$ on \emph{Chain}-loose; per-component
concessions within $\pm 4$ units), and dominates at $R{=}20$ and
$R{=}30$ ($+15\%$ to $+37\%$, with large negative
$\Delta m$ and $\Delta D$ --- hundreds of objective units in absolute terms).
Two ingredients hold the zero-movement packing at or near the certified
optima: a nest-stretch candidate that reproduces the optimum's
stay-stretching mechanism over the actual blocking digraph
(\S\ref{sec:igvnd_safety}), and
the random half of the destruction step, which keeps the walk from anchoring
in one basin.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The two methods are complementary rather than competing.  The MILP is the
instrument of record on instances it can close: it certifies optimality on
the small and loosely constrained cases, and it is the yardstick that lets
us \emph{measure} the heuristic's quality there --- where IGVND is shown to
be exact or near-exact.  At $R{=}10$ its timed-out incumbents remain
competitive on the blocking topologies.  IGVND is the instrument of choice
at operational scale: on every configuration at $R{=}20$ and beyond, the
heuristic delivers a markedly better feasible schedule in the same 60\,s,
with a guaranteed-feasible zero-movement floor and a checker-validated
manoeuvre-aware polish.  Read jointly, the MILP validates the heuristic on
the tractable end of the benchmark and the heuristic extends usable
solution quality to the intractable end --- which is the regime an
operator actually faces.
