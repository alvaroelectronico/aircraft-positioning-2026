\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
% ==============================================================================

We have studied an aircraft hangar positioning problem in which blocking
interactions depend on the per-job timing of the front aircraft rather than
on a single block of occupancy.  We contributed an
exact MILP formulation whose partition constraint classifies every access
into one of four admissible states and correctly forbids accesses that
would fall inside a non-interruptible job, and a metaheuristic, IGVND, that
separates the combinatorial assignment-and-ordering decision from the
timing decision and prices the former with a two-regime decoder under an
Iterated-Greedy / VND search with a checker-validated safety net.

The experimental comparison on 270 instances delivers a clear and honest
picture.  On the tractable end of the benchmark --- small fleets,
no or loose blocking --- the MILP proves optimality and IGVND matches it,
being exact on the no-blocking and five-aircraft instances and within a
few percent on the remaining converged cases; the lone large relative
gap is a small-denominator artefact that vanishes in the per-component
view.  A structural gap identified against the certified optima --- the
optimum stretches aircraft stays with inter-job idle to nest tighter, a
schedule shape outside the tight-packing decoder's reach --- was closed by
folding the same mechanism into the heuristic as an explicit,
checker-validated candidate over the blocking digraph; the heuristic now
attains or beats the certified optimum on all but two seeds of that
family, in several cases ending below the MILP's integer-gridded
objective.  On the intractable end --- larger fleets and the profiles that
activate the manoeuvre machinery --- the MILP no longer converges within the
budget, and IGVND returns markedly better feasible solutions in the same
60\,s, by up to $+44\%$ and hundreds of objective units in absolute terms;
at the largest scale the exact solver even exhausts memory and returns no
solution at all on instances the heuristic solves without difficulty.  The
two methods are therefore complementary: the MILP certifies quality where it
can, and the heuristic extends usable solution quality to the operational
scale where exact optimisation times out --- or fails outright.

Several directions remain open.  The MILP could be tightened with
valid inequalities or a branch-and-cut treatment of the partition
constraint to push the tractability frontier outward.  On the heuristic
side, the remaining residuals --- two seeds of one configuration family and
the gaps at scale --- are at the level of the time-limited search's
run-to-run variance, so the next lever is search throughput (an
incremental, prefix-reusing decoder) rather than new mechanisms.  Finally,
the present study
fixes a single 60\,s budget and three corner weight profiles; an anytime
profile of solution quality versus time, and a sweep over intermediate
weight combinations, would further characterise the operating envelope of
each method.
