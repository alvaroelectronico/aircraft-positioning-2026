\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
% ==============================================================================

We have studied an aircraft hangar positioning problem in which blocking
interactions depend on the per-job timing of the front aircraft rather than
on a single block of occupancy.  We contributed an
exact MILP formulation whose partition constraint classifies every access
into one of four admissible states and correctly forbids accesses that
would fall inside a non-interruptible job, and a metaheuristic, IGVND, that
separates the combinatorial assignment-and-ordering decision from the
timing decision and prices the former with a two-regime decoder under an
Iterated-Greedy / VND search with a checker-validated safety net.

The experimental comparison on 370 instances delivers a clear and honest
picture.  On the tractable end of the benchmark --- small fleets,
no or loose blocking --- the MILP proves optimality and IGVND matches it,
exactly on the no-blocking baseline and on the large majority of
five-aircraft seeds (all 90 of them under movement-priority), conceding
only a few objective units elsewhere; the large relative gaps in that
regime are
small-denominator artefacts that vanish in the per-component view.  A
structural gap identified against the certified optima --- the
optimum stretches aircraft stays with inter-job idle to nest tighter, a
schedule shape outside the tight-packing decoder's reach --- was narrowed by
folding the same mechanism into the heuristic as an explicit,
checker-validated candidate over the blocking digraph; the remaining
concessions to certified optima are a few makespan units on the
\emph{Chain} topology at $R{=}10$ and a couple of delay units on
scattered small-instance seeds.  On the intractable end --- larger fleets and the profiles that
activate the manoeuvre machinery --- the MILP no longer converges within the
budget, and IGVND returns markedly better feasible solutions in the same
60\,s, by up to $+44\%$ and hundreds to thousands of objective units in
absolute terms.  The
two methods are therefore complementary: the MILP certifies quality where it
can, and the heuristic extends usable solution quality to the operational
scale where exact optimisation times out.

Several directions remain open.  The MILP could be tightened with
valid inequalities, pair-dependent big-$M$ constants derived from the
projected time windows of each aircraft pair, or a branch-and-cut
treatment of the partition constraint, to push the tractability frontier
outward.  On the heuristic
side, the remaining residuals --- a few conceded objective units on the
converged small cells, and the makespan-priority concession on
\emph{Chain} at $R{=}10$ --- point to search
throughput (an incremental, prefix-reusing decoder) as the next lever
rather than new mechanisms.  Finally,
the present study
fixes a single 60\,s budget, three corner weight profiles and a handful of
a-priori search parameters (such as the shuffle bias $\beta$); an anytime
profile of solution quality versus time, a sweep over intermediate
weight combinations, and a sensitivity analysis of those parameters would
further characterise the operating envelope of each method.  Learning-based
dispatching policies are an increasingly popular alternative for real-time
scheduling~\citep{Bengio2021}; in the setting studied here, where a single
misclassified access renders a schedule infeasible, we expect model-based
search around an exact feasibility oracle to remain the safer backbone,
with learned guidance --- for instance, steering the destruction step or
the insertion order --- as a natural hybridisation to explore.
