% ==============================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
% ==============================================================================

We have studied an aircraft hangar positioning problem in which blocking
interactions depend on the per-job timing of the front aircraft rather than
on a single block of occupancy.  We contributed an exact MILP formulation
whose partition constraint classifies every access into one of four
admissible states and excludes accesses inside non-interruptible jobs,
and a metaheuristic, IGVND, that searches over position assignments and
priority orders, generates schedules with a two-regime decoder, and
adjusts per-aircraft timing so that rear accesses coincide with
boundaries of the front aircraft that block them.

On the benchmark, the MILP certifies optimality on small fleets and
loosely constrained cases, where IGVND attains the certified optimum in
the large majority of cases, and the heuristic returns the better
feasible solution within the same time budget on every larger instance.
The per-aircraft timing adjustment is the component that lets the
decoder reproduce the optima in which a rear aircraft is delayed, or its
stay stretched, so that its access coincides with a boundary of the
front aircraft that blocks it.

Future work includes solving an exact timing subproblem over a fixed
assignment and order to reach the optima that need several aircraft
shifted jointly, and characterising the heuristic over the time budget
and over intermediate weight combinations.
