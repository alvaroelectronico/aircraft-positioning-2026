\section{Literature review}
\label{sec:lit_review}
% ==============================================================================
In this section, we position our problem within the aircraft maintenance scheduling literature.

Following the classification proposed by \citet{van2013aircraft}, aircraft maintenance scheduling problems can be divided into two levels according to \emph{where} maintenance is carried out: (i) line maintenance scheduling, referring to maintenance that can be performed when an aircraft parks at the gate or at the apron, and (ii) hangar scheduling, which optimizes the spatial-temporal placement of accepted maintenance requests inside a single hangar.

Research on line maintenance scheduling \citep{rodrigo_line_maintenance_2026} allocates maintenance tasks to the ground times available between flights while coordinating task timing and resource availability, from single-base integer programming models with deterministic processing times \citep{safaei2011workforce} to the multi-base Line Maintenance Scheduling Problem of \citet{Shaukat2020}, its decomposition into elementary activities with precedence and synchronisation relationships \citep{SCIAU2024102537}, and extensions with uncertain processing times and their propagation through the flight network \citep{Shahmoradi-Moghadam2021,VILLAFRANCA2025104012}.

Hangar scheduling, by contrast, starts from accepted maintenance requests and determines their timing and placement within the facility, so the within-hangar geometry introduces a spatial dimension absent from line maintenance. \citet{Qin2017} introduced this dimension by incorporating aircraft geometry into a MILP coupling maintenance scheduling with multi-period parking layouts, aircraft non-overlap and movement blocking. Subsequent work selected the requests to serve and optimised the layout for safety margins \citep{Qin2018}, strengthened with valid inequalities derived from infeasible layouts \citep{Qin2018b}; added time-varying capacity and the blocking of movement paths, solved by an event-based MILP and a rolling-horizon heuristic \citep{Qin2019}; integrated multi-skill technician assignment through a two-stage decomposition \citep{Qin2020}; and moved to continuous time with substantial computational gains over event-based formulations \citep{Pazhooh2025}.

In this literature, the feasibility of accessing a blocked position does not depend on which maintenance task the obstructing aircraft is performing at that instant. Even when individual tasks are explicitly scheduled, as in \citet{Qin2020}, access is governed by the occupancy of the blocking position alone; conversely, \citet{Pileggi2026} scheduled individual tasks with durations, precedence relationships and shared resources, but without connecting them to the spatial configuration of aircraft within the hangar. Here, access feasibility and towing requirements depend on whether the obstructing aircraft is between jobs, performing an interruptible job or performing a non-interruptible job.
