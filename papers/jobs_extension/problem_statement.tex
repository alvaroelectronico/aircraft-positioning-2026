\section{Problem statement}
\label{sec:problem}
% ==============================================================================

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{Operational overview}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A maintenance hangar consists of a finite number of parking positions.
Because of the geometry of the facility, some positions are \emph{rear}
positions whose only access path runs across one or more \emph{front}
positions, so an aircraft parked at a front position obstructs the entry
and exit of any aircraft behind it; whenever a rear aircraft must enter
or leave while the front position is occupied, the front aircraft is
towed out of the way and returned once the manoeuvre is complete.  A
fleet of aircraft must be serviced over a planning horizon.  Each
aircraft has an earliest start, a target finish whose violation incurs a
delay cost, and an ordered chain of \emph{jobs} with known durations,
each tagged as \emph{interruptible}, meaning it can be paused briefly to
allow a neighbouring manoeuvre and resumed afterwards, or
\emph{non-interruptible}.  A schedule assigns each aircraft to a
position and fixes the start of each of its jobs.  Aircraft sharing a
position are serviced one after the other, and a rear aircraft can enter
or leave freely when the front position is unoccupied, while an occupied
front position allows the manoeuvre only during an inter-job gap of the
front aircraft or by pausing one of its interruptible jobs
(Section~\ref{subsec:access}).  The objective is a weighted combination of
makespan, total delay and towing movements.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{Formal definition}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The hangar is modelled as a directed acyclic graph $G = (\calP, \calA)$
where $\calP = \{p_1, \dots, p_P\}$ is the set of parking positions and
$\calA \subseteq \calP \times \calP$ is the set of \emph{blocking
arcs}: an arc $(p, p') \in \calA$ means that position $p$ (the
\emph{front}) obstructs the access path to position $p'$ (the
\emph{rear}).

Let $\calR = \{r_1, \dots, r_R\}$ be the fleet of aircraft to be
serviced.  Each aircraft $r \in \calR$ carries an ordered chain of jobs
$\calJ_r = (j^r_1, \ldots, j^r_{N_r})$, in which $j^r_{k+1}$ may only
start after $j^r_k$ has finished, and
$\calJ = \bigcup_{r \in \calR} \calJ_r$ is the global job set.  Each
job $j \in \calJ$ has a nominal duration $D_j > 0$ and an
interruptibility flag $I_j \in \{0, 1\}$, with $I_j = 1$ if and only if
$j$ is interruptible.  The aggregate processing time of aircraft $r$ is
$T_r = \sum_{j \in \calJ_r} D_j$, and each aircraft also has an earliest
start $E_r \ge 0$ and a target finish $L_r > E_r$.

Three positive temporal parameters, specified per instance, complete
the data.  The towing time $\varepsilon$ is the time needed to move an
aircraft into or out of a parking slot, and acts as the minimum
separation between two consecutive aircraft serviced at the same
position.  The gap requirement $\mu$ is the operational time absorbed by
each access manoeuvre performed in an inter-job gap of the front
aircraft: a gap must be at least $\mu$ times the number of manoeuvres
it hosts, and may be zero if it hosts none.  The interruption penalty
$\delta$ is the additional working time added to an interruptible job
each time it is paused to allow a neighbouring access manoeuvre.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
%\subsection*{Decision variables and basic constraints}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A solution assigns each aircraft $r \in \calR$ to exactly one position
$\pi(r) \in \calP$, and fixes a start time $s_j \ge 0$ for every job
$j \in \calJ$, whose finish time is then
$f_j = s_j + D_j + \delta\, \kappa_j$, where $\kappa_j \in \NZ$ counts
how many times job $j$ was paused during its execution to enable a
neighbouring access manoeuvre (see the access conditions below), with
$\kappa_j > 0$ requiring $I_j = 1$.  The job start and finish times
induce the aircraft start and finish times $s_r = s_{j^r_1}$ and
$f_r = f_{j^r_{N_r}}$, and the per-aircraft delay
$v^D_r = \max(0,\, f_r - L_r)$.

A feasible solution must satisfy four basic requirements: each aircraft is
assigned to exactly one position; the jobs of an aircraft run in their
prescribed order, each starting no earlier than its predecessor finishes;
no aircraft starts before its earliest start $E_r$; and two aircraft that
share a position are serviced sequentially, with disjoint occupancy
intervals $[s_r, f_r]$ and $[s_{r'}, f_{r'}]$ separated by at least the tow
time $\varepsilon$.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
% \subsubsection*{Access conditions on blocked positions}
\label{subsec:access}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consider any arc $(p, p') \in \calA$ and an aircraft $r'$ assigned to
the rear position $p' = \pi(r')$.  Its two access instants are the
entry $\tau = s_{r'}$ and the exit $\tau = f_{r'}$.  Each of these
instants must fall into exactly one of three modes, depending on the
state of the front position $p$ at time $\tau$.

\emph{Mode~A.}  The front position is vacant at $\tau$: no aircraft $r$
with $\pi(r) = p$ satisfies $s_r \le \tau \le f_r$, so the access needs
no manoeuvre and incurs no extra time and no movement count.

\emph{Mode~B.}  The front aircraft is mid-stay and $\tau$ falls in an
inter-job gap: some aircraft $r$ with $\pi(r) = p$ has
$f_{j^r_k} \le \tau \le s_{j^r_{k+1}}$ between two consecutive jobs.
The front aircraft is towed out, $r'$ accesses $p'$, and it is towed
back; the gap must satisfy $s_{j^r_{k+1}} - f_{j^r_k} \ge \mu$ whenever
an access instant uses it.  The event contributes $2$ to the movement
count (tow-out plus tow-back) and does not extend any job.

\emph{Mode~C.}  The front aircraft is mid-job on an interruptible job:
some aircraft $r$ with $\pi(r) = p$ has $s_{j^r_k} < \tau < f_{j^r_k}$
strictly inside a job $j^r_k$, and the access is feasible only if
$I_{j^r_k} = 1$.  The job is then paused, the manoeuvre performed, and
the job resumed; its counter $\kappa_{j^r_k}$ increases by $1$ and the
event contributes $2$ to the movement count.

An access that meets the Mode~C timing on a \emph{non-interruptible} job
($I_{j^r_k} = 0$) fits none of the three modes and is therefore infeasible:
the schedule must retime $r'$ so that the instant falls into Mode~A or
Mode~B, or reassign $r'$ to another position.  The entry $s_{r'}$ and the
exit $f_{r'}$ are classified independently, against potentially different
front aircraft and jobs, and each contributes its own movement count and,
in Mode~C, its own increment of $\kappa$.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
% \subsubsection*{Objective}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let $m = \max_{r \in \calR} f_r$ be the makespan and let $n$ be the total
number of movements summed over all blocking arcs and aircraft pairs.  The
objective is a weighted combination of three criteria --- the makespan $m$,
the total delay $\sum_{r \in \calR} v^D_r$, and the movement count $n$ ---
scalarised with user-specified weights $W^M, W^D, W^S \ge 0$ that express
the relative priority of throughput, on-time delivery, and ground-crew
effort; the resulting cost is minimised.  The formal objective is stated
with the model as~\eqref{eq:milpobj}.

The problem is $\mathcal{NP}$-hard: even without blocking arcs it
contains the single-machine scheduling problem with arbitrary release
dates and weighted completion times~\citep{Lenstra1977}, and the
blocking access conditions of Section~\ref{subsec:access} further enlarge
the combinatorial structure.  The mixed-integer programming
formulation that captures these conditions is developed in
Section~\ref{sec:milp}.
