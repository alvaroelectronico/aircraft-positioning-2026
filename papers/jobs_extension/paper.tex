\title[Aircraft Hangar Positioning: Job-Level Extension]{Aircraft Hangar
Positioning with Job-Level Blocking: A Mixed-Integer Programming Extension}

\author*[1]{\fnm{\'Alvaro} \sur{Garc\'ia}}
\email{alvaro.garcia@upm.es}

\affil*[1]{\orgname{Universidad Polit\'ecnica de Madrid},
           \orgaddress{\city{Madrid}, \country{Spain}}}

\abstract{Maintenance hangars are blocking resources: an aircraft at a
front position obstructs the access path of the aircraft behind it, and
clearing that path may require towing the front aircraft out and back, an
expensive manoeuvre.  We study this problem at the \emph{job level}, where
whether a rear aircraft can be accessed depends on which job the front
aircraft is running --- an inter-job gap admits a cheap manoeuvre, an
interruptible job admits one at the cost of a bounded extension, and a
non-interruptible job admits none.  We contribute (i) an exact
mixed-integer linear programming formulation whose partition constraint
classifies every access into one of four admissible states and renders
the impossible ones infeasible, and (ii) IGVND, an Iterated-Greedy /
Variable-Neighbourhood-Descent metaheuristic that separates the
combinatorial assignment-and-ordering decision from the timing decision and
prices it with a fast two-regime decoder under a checker-validated safety
net.  On a controlled 270-instance benchmark spanning five blocking
topologies, four fleet sizes and three weight profiles, the heuristic
matches the MILP wherever the latter proves optimality (exact on the
no-blocking and five-aircraft instances, within a couple of percent
elsewhere) and returns substantially better feasible solutions in the same
60\,s budget on every configuration the MILP cannot close --- larger
fleets, dense topologies, and the profiles that activate the manoeuvre
machinery.}

\keywords{Aircraft hangar scheduling,
          Job-level mixed-integer linear programming,
          Blocking constraints,
          Maintenance planning,
          Resource-constrained project scheduling}
