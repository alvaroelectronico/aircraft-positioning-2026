\abstract{Scheduled maintenance keeps an airline's fleet airworthy, and
much of it is carried out in hangars whose parking capacity is limited and
whose layout constrains how aircraft move in and out.  Such hangars are
blocking resources: an aircraft at a front position obstructs the access
path of the aircraft behind it, and clearing that path may require towing
the front aircraft out and back, an expensive manoeuvre.  Whether a rear
aircraft can be accessed while a front aircraft is being serviced depends
on which of the front aircraft's jobs is running: an access during an
inter-job gap requires sufficient idle time but does not extend any job,
an access during an interruptible job adds a bounded amount of processing
time, and an access during a non-interruptible job is not admissible.  We
contribute (i) an exact mixed-integer linear programming formulation whose
partition constraint classifies every access into one of four admissible
states and excludes inadmissible accesses, and (ii) IGVND, an Iterated-Greedy
metaheuristic with a neighbourhood-descent local search that separates
assignment and sequencing from schedule generation through a two-regime
decoder.  On a controlled 370-instance benchmark spanning four blocking
topologies, four fleet sizes and three slack levels, the MILP proves
optimality on the small instances and IGVND matches the certified
optimal objective value in 404 of 439 cases; on the larger instances, which the MILP cannot close
within the 60\,s budget, IGVND finds better feasible solutions than the
MILP solver in every run with 20 or 30 aircraft.}
