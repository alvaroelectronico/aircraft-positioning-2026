\abstract{Aircraft hangar maintenance scheduling requires coordinating
maintenance jobs with aircraft positioning and access. Existing approaches
do not fully capture how access to an aircraft whose path is obstructed by
another depends on the maintenance job being performed by the obstructing
aircraft. We address this gap by jointly modelling maintenance jobs at
sufficient granularity to distinguish their interruption characteristics
and a hangar layout that captures the complexity of accessing jobs on
aircraft whose paths are obstructed. The feasibility and cost of access
manoeuvres depend on whether the obstructing aircraft is between jobs,
undergoing an interruptible job, or undergoing a non-interruptible job.
The objective balances makespan, total delay, and manoeuvres. We develop an
exact continuous-time mixed-integer linear programming formulation that
partitions blocked accesses into mutually exclusive admissible states, and
a metaheuristic combining iterated greedy search with neighbourhood descent.
The heuristic separates position assignment and aircraft ordering from
schedule construction, and adjusts job timing to align accesses with
feasible boundaries. Computational experiments cover 370 instances with
different blocking layouts, fleet sizes, and time-window slack levels,
evaluated under three objective weight profiles with a common 60-second
budget. The heuristic matches 404 of 439 certified optimal objective values.
For fleets of 20 and 30 aircraft, it improves on the mathematical solver's
feasible solution in all 540 comparisons, with mean objective reductions of
up to 52.9\% across experimental configurations. These results demonstrate
the heuristic's effectiveness on larger instances, where it consistently
delivers better schedules than the exact solver within the same
computational budget.}
