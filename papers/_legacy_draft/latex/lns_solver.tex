\section{Large Neighbourhood Search (LNS)}
\label{sec:lns}

\subsection{Overview}

The LNS solver (\texttt{solvers/lns\_solver.py}) iterates a
\textbf{destroy–repair–accept} cycle.  Starting from a good initial solution,
it repeatedly removes a subset of aircraft (\emph{destroy}), re-optimises
their position assignments (\emph{repair}), and accepts the result if it
improves the incumbent.

Three repair operators are available:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \emph{Exhaustive repair} — enumerate all $|\mathcal{P}|^k$
          combinations when $k \leq k_{\text{exact}}$.
    \item \emph{GRASP repair} — $n_{\text{grasp\_repair}}$ randomised
          re-insertions for high iteration throughput.
    \item \emph{Sub-MILP repair} — compact \texttt{gurobipy} model solved
          exactly for the $k$ free aircraft; yields maximum quality per call.
\end{itemize}

The destroy strategy is selected either statically (e.g.\ \texttt{mixed},
\texttt{cluster}) or \emph{adaptively} via \textbf{ALNS} (Adaptive LNS),
which maintains per-strategy weights updated by a reward signal after each
iteration.

\subsection{Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Large Neighbourhood Search (LNS / ALNS)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\If{\texttt{warm\_solution} provided}
    \State $s_0 \leftarrow \texttt{warm\_solution}$   \Comment{e.g.\ from TopologyHeuristic}
\Else
    \State $s_0 \leftarrow \text{ConstructiveHeuristic}(t_{\text{init}})$
\EndIf
\State $s^* \leftarrow \text{LocalSearch}(s_0,\; \texttt{max\_passes} = \max(5,\lfloor R/2\rfloor))$
\State $s_{\text{cur}} \leftarrow s^*$
\If{destroy $=$ \texttt{alns}}
    \State Initialise weights $w_d \leftarrow 1$ for each $d \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{alns}}$
\EndIf
\While{time budget not exhausted}
    \State $k \sim \mathcal{U}[k_{\min}, k_{\max}]$
    \State $d^* \leftarrow$ selected destroy strategy (roulette if ALNS, else static)
    \State $\mathcal{R}_{\text{del}} \leftarrow \text{Destroy}(s_{\text{cur}}, k, d^*)$
    \If{$k \leq k_{\text{exact}}$}
        \State $s \leftarrow \text{ExhaustiveRepair}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{del}})$
    \ElsIf{gurobipy available \textbf{and} \texttt{repair} $\in$ \{\texttt{submilp}, \texttt{auto}\}}
        \State $s \leftarrow \text{SubMILPRepair}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{del}})$
        \If{$s = \emptyset$} $s \leftarrow \text{GRASPRepair}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{del}})$ \EndIf
    \Else
        \State $s \leftarrow \text{GRASPRepair}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{del}})$
    \EndIf
    \State $s_{\text{rb}} \leftarrow \text{Rebuild}(s)$   \Comment{greedy schedule from position assignment}
    \If{$z(s_{\text{rb}}) < z(s_{\text{cur}})$}   \Comment{compare rebuild quality — not trial quality}
        \State $s_{\text{cur}} \leftarrow s_{\text{rb}}$
        \If{$z(s_{\text{rb}}) < z(s^*)$}
            \State $s^* \leftarrow \text{LocalSearch}(s_{\text{rb}})$   \Comment{convergent polish on global best}
            \State Update ALNS weight: global-best reward
        \Else
            \State Update ALNS weight: local-improvement reward
        \EndIf
    \Else
        \State Update ALNS weight: no-improvement reward
    \EndIf
    \If{no improvement for \texttt{restart\_every} iterations}
        \State $s_{\text{cur}} \leftarrow \text{ConstructiveHeuristic}()$
              + $\text{LocalSearch}(\cdot,\;\texttt{max\_passes})$
    \EndIf
\EndWhile
\State \Return $s^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Accept correctness.}
The repair step may produce a trial solution $s$ whose objective is computed
from an optimised schedule (GRASP or Gurobi).  The actual schedule stored in
$s_{\text{cur}}$ is obtained by \texttt{Rebuild}, a greedy procedure that
processes aircraft in earliest-start order.  Because Rebuild may yield a
different (potentially worse) objective than the trial, the accept condition
uses \emph{Rebuild quality}, not trial quality.  Using the trial quality for
acceptance but storing the Rebuild result would silently degrade $s_{\text{cur}}$
over many iterations.

\subsection{Initialisation}

Two warm-start modes are supported:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{External warm solution.}  If \texttt{warm\_solution} is
          passed in \texttt{params}, it is used directly as the starting point.
          This allows, for example, the topology heuristic to seed the LNS
          with a high-quality initial solution rather than spending budget on
          an internal constructive run.
    \item \textbf{Internal constructive run.}  Otherwise, the LNS spends
          $t_{\text{init}} = \min(10\text{ s},\; 0.15 \cdot t_{\text{limit}})$
          running the Constructive Heuristic (Section~\ref{sec:constructive})
          with $\alpha = 0.7$.
\end{enumerate}

After obtaining $s_0$, a \textbf{bounded local search} is applied with
$\texttt{max\_passes} = \max(5,\; \lfloor R/2 \rfloor)$.  The cap prevents
the initial polish from consuming the entire time budget on large instances
(e.g.\ $R=30$, where an unbounded convergent search can take tens of seconds).
The same bound is applied to local searches triggered by restarts.

\subsection{Destroy Strategies}
\label{sec:destroy}

The destroy operator selects $k$ aircraft to remove from the current solution.
Six strategies are available:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lp{8.5cm}}
\toprule
Strategy & Description \\
\midrule
\texttt{worst} & Remove the $k$ aircraft with the largest individual delays
    $\Delta_r$. \\
\texttt{most\_blocking} & Score each aircraft by the number of active blocking
    arcs it participates in; remove the $k$ highest-scoring aircraft. \\
\texttt{random} & Uniform random selection. \\
\texttt{mixed} & Use \texttt{most\_blocking} when total delay $\approx 0$;
    otherwise use \texttt{worst}. \\
\texttt{cluster} & Space-time cluster destroy
    (see Section~\ref{sec:cluster-destroy}). \\
\texttt{mixed\_cluster} & Use \texttt{most\_blocking} when total delay $\approx 0$;
    otherwise use \texttt{cluster}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsubsection{Adaptive LNS (\texttt{destroy = alns})}
\label{sec:alns}

When \texttt{destroy = alns}, the solver maintains a weight $w_d \geq 0$ for
each strategy $d$ in
$\mathcal{D}_{\text{alns}} = \{\texttt{worst},\;\texttt{most\_blocking},\;\texttt{cluster}\}$.
At the start of each iteration, a strategy is sampled by roulette:
\begin{equation}
    P(\text{select } d) = \frac{w_d}{\sum_{d'} w_{d'}}
    \label{eq:alns-roulette}
\end{equation}
After each iteration, the weight of the selected strategy is updated with
exponential smoothing:
\begin{equation}
    w_d \;\leftarrow\; (1 - \lambda)\,w_d \;+\; \lambda\,\rho
    \label{eq:alns-update}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda = 0.85$ is the learning rate and $\rho$ is the reward:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Event & Reward $\rho$ \\
\midrule
Repair improved global best $s^*$ & 3.0 \\
Repair improved current solution only & 1.0 \\
No improvement (rebuild $\geq s_{\text{cur}}$) & 0.0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All weights are initialised to $1.0$.  Final weights are printed at the end of
each run for diagnostics.

\subsubsection{Space-Time Cluster Destroy (\texttt{cluster})}
\label{sec:cluster-destroy}

The \texttt{cluster} strategy identifies a real bottleneck — a specific place
and time where several aircraft compete for the same corridor — and destroys
the entire involved neighbourhood simultaneously.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Space-Time Cluster Destroy}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Seed selection (roulette)}:
    weight $r$ as $(\Delta_r + 1) \cdot (\text{bs}(r) + 1)$;
    sample one aircraft $r^*$.
\State \textbf{Spatial cluster}:
    $\mathcal{P}^* \leftarrow \{p_{r^*}\}
    \cup \{p' : (p_{r^*}, p') \in \mathcal{B}\}
    \cup \{p' : (p', p_{r^*}) \in \mathcal{B}\}$
\State \textbf{Temporal filter} with margin $= 0.5 \cdot \bar{D}$:
    $\mathcal{C} \leftarrow \{r \in \mathcal{R}
    : p_r \in \mathcal{P}^*
    \;\wedge\; s_r < t_{f}^* + \text{margin}
    \;\wedge\; f_r > t_{s}^* - \text{margin}\}$
\State Rank $\mathcal{C}$ by $(\Delta_r + \text{bs}(r))$ descending; take top $k$.
\State If $|\mathcal{C}| < k$, fill remainder with highest-$\Delta_r$ aircraft outside $\mathcal{C}$.
\State \Return $k$ selected aircraft IDs.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Repair Operators}

\subsubsection{Exhaustive Repair ($k \leq k_{\text{exact}}$)}

All $|\mathcal{P}|^k$ position combinations are enumerated.  For each
combination, a complete schedule is rebuilt (\texttt{\_rebuild}) and the
combination minimising the objective is selected.  For default settings
($k_{\text{exact}} = 4$, $|\mathcal{P}| = 5$) this evaluates at most
$5^4 = 625$ combinations per iteration.

\subsubsection{GRASP Repair ($k > k_{\text{exact}}$, \texttt{repair = grasp})}

$n_{\text{grasp\_repair}}$ (default 12) random trials are performed.  Each
trial uses the GRASP construction from Section~\ref{sec:constructive} to
re-insert $\mathcal{R}_{\text{free}}$ into the partial schedule defined by
$\mathcal{R}_{\text{fix}}$.  The best trial is kept.

\subsubsection{Sub-MILP Repair ($k > k_{\text{exact}}$, \texttt{repair = submilp / auto})}
\label{sec:lns-submilp}

When Gurobi is available, the sub-MILP repair replaces GRASP repair with an
\emph{exact} solve of a compact subproblem.  A fresh \texttt{gurobipy} model
is built containing only the $k$ free aircraft; fixed aircraft appear as
time-window constraints on position occupancy intervals.  A module-level
\texttt{gurobipy.Env} is created once at import time to amortise the
$\sim$0.5\,s startup cost.

\paragraph{Fallback monitoring.}
After each run, the solver prints the sub-MILP call statistics:
\begin{center}
\texttt{sub-MILP calls=N  feasible=M (XX\%)  fallback\_grasp=K}
\end{center}
If the feasibility rate falls below 50\%, a warning is emitted recommending
a reduction of \texttt{k\_max} or an increase of \texttt{repair\_time\_s}.

\subsection{Schedule Rebuild}

Given a position assignment $\{(r, p_r)\}_{r \in \mathcal{R}}$,
\texttt{\_rebuild} computes start times by processing aircraft in
earliest-start order (or an explicit order if provided — used by the
intra-position adjacent-swap in local search):
\[
    t_s(r) = \max\bigl(e_r,\; \text{pos\_free}[p] + \delta,\;
    t_s^{\text{block}}\bigr)
\]

\subsection{Local Search}

Two invocations of the local search occur:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Initial and restart polish:} bounded search with
          $\texttt{max\_passes} = \max(5, \lfloor R/2 \rfloor)$.
          Prevents the first polish from consuming the entire time budget on
          large instances.
    \item \textbf{Global-best polish:} convergent search (unbounded), fired
          only when a repair strictly improves the global best $s^*$.
          Repairs that only improve $s_{\text{cur}}$ are accepted without
          local search, preserving iteration budget.
\end{enumerate}

Both invocations use single-move and 2-opt swap operators (first-improvement).
