\section{Experiment Series 04: Strict-$\delta$ Baselines}
\label{sec:exp04}

\subsection{Motivation}

All results in Series~01--03 used an inconsistent feasibility definition:
the Pyomo MILP did not enforce the minimum separation $\delta=10$\,min between
consecutive aircraft at the same position, while the topology heuristic and
FAS did.  This made direct objective comparisons invalid.

Two fixes were applied on 2026-05-14:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{RQ08 added to \texttt{check\_solution.py}}: verifies
          $S_{r'} \geq F_r + \delta$ for every consecutive same-position pair.
          Audit confirmed \texttt{topology\_ms6} passes RQ08 on all instances
          ($\min\,\text{gap} = 10.00$ exactly).  The MILP baseline (pre-fix)
          would have violated RQ08.
    \item \textbf{MILP Pyomo corrected}: two new constraints added to
          \texttt{models/milp\_pyomo.py} (see equations~\eqref{eq:same-pos-delta-1}
          and~\eqref{eq:same-pos-delta-2} in Section~\ref{sec:milp}):
          \begin{align*}
              S_{r'} &\geq F_r + \delta - M(1-q_{rr'p}) - M(1-x_{rp}) - M(1-x_{r'p}) \\
              S_r    &\geq F_{r'} + \delta - M(1-q_{r'rp}) - M(1-x_{rp}) - M(1-x_{r'p})
          \end{align*}
          The model size increases by $2|\mathcal{A}^2 \times \mathcal{P}|$
          rows (e.g.\ 14326 → 14776 for $R=10$, 45271 → 47171 for $R=20$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Configuration}

Shared: $w_M=0.1$, $w_D=1.0$, $w_V=10$, $t_{\text{limit}}=60$\,s,
MIPGap=0, $\delta=10$, $\alpha=0.3$, $w_{\text{topo}}=1$, seed=1.

All four methods (\texttt{milp\_baseline}, \texttt{topology\_ms6},
\texttt{tgr\_k5}, \texttt{fas\_on\_topo}) now operate under the same
feasibility definition.

\subsection{Analysis}
\label{exp:exp04-analysis}

\paragraph{Impact of correcting $\delta$ in the MILP baseline.}

The MILP baseline objective increases substantially with the $\delta$ correction:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Instance & Old (no $\delta$) & New (strict $\delta$) \\
\midrule
R=10 & 4.79 (opt) & 5.87 (opt) \\
R=20 & 200.78 (tLim) & 334.75 (tLim) \\
R=30 & aborted & aborted \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For R=20, the strict MILP is dramatically harder: the incumbent found within
60\,s is 334.75, worse than \texttt{topology\_ms6} at 236.06.  This is
expected: the $\delta$ separation creates tight sequencing constraints with
many aircraft per position, making the MIP tree much harder to prune.

\paragraph{Topology heuristic is now the strongest method for R=20/30.}

Under strict $\delta$, \texttt{topology\_ms6} (236.06 for R=20, 225.51 for
R=30) is the best feasible method within 60\,s for medium and large instances.
The MILP baseline no longer provides a useful upper reference for R$\geq$20.

\paragraph{FAS on topology assignment: R=20 regression resolved.}

In Series~03 (pre-fix), \texttt{fas\_on\_topo} gave 253.59 vs topology's
242.86 for R=20 — apparently worse.  Under strict $\delta$, topology gives
236.06 and \texttt{fas\_on\_topo} gives 247.86 — still worse, confirming
that the regression persists.  Root cause: FAS models blocking arcs at
aircraft-block level ($D_r = \sum d_j$), which is more conservative than
the topology heuristic's job-by-job blocking evaluation.  This is a
modelling gap in FAS, not a feasibility bug (see
Section~\ref{sec:fas-modelling-diff}).

\paragraph{R=30: fas\_on\_topo nearly matches topology\_ms6.}

Under strict $\delta$, \texttt{fas\_on\_topo} achieves 224.24 vs
topology's 225.51 for R=30 — effectively equal, with FAS not yet proven
optimal within 60\,s.  FAS uses the same position assignment as the
topology solution; any remaining gap is due to the blocking-arc conservatism
noted above.

\paragraph{Small instances: all methods find the same optimum.}

For R$\leq$5, all four methods achieve the same objective under strict $\delta$,
consistent with prior results.

\subsection{Root Cause of fas\_on\_topo $>$ topology on R=20}
\label{exp:exp04-fas-gap}

Code audit (2026-05-14) found that the gap is \emph{not} due to job-level vs
aircraft-level blocking windows — both solvers use $D_r = \sum_j d_j$.
The actual cause is a movement-counting discrepancy:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \texttt{\_count\_movements} (checker + heuristic cost): strict
          inequalities $S_r < S_{r'} < F_r$ and $S_r < F_{r'} < F_r$.
    \item FAS $u^{\mathrm{in}}$ indicator: $S_r \leq S_{r'} \leq F_r - \delta$
          (non-strict at lower bound, extra $-\delta$ at upper bound).
\end{itemize}

Consequence: FAS optimises for its own movement count (not the checker's).
With $w_V = 10$, FAS may add up to 20 units of extra delay to avoid one
``ghost'' movement that the checker would not count.

Both solutions in Series~04 show $\texttt{mov}=0$ by the checker, but FAS
chose a different aircraft ordering at shared positions to avoid its
internal movement penalties, increasing delay (topology=238.95 vs
fas=245.00 for R=20 in this run).

\subsection{Next Steps}
\label{exp:exp04-next}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Pipeline seguro topology$\to$FAS} (implemented 2026-05-14):
          return $\min(\text{topology}, \text{fas\_on\_topo})$.
          Guarantees FAS never degrades the result regardless of counting
          differences.  See Series~05.
    \item \textbf{$|C_r|=2$ selectivo} (implemented 2026-05-14):
          extend FAS to accept a list of position candidates per aircraft.
          Candidates = union of positions across 10 \texttt{generate\_assignments}
          runs.  Attacks the residual gap R=10.  See Series~05.
    \item \textbf{Align FAS movement counting with checker}: use strict
          inequalities and remove $-\delta$ from upper bound of $\beta^{\mathrm{in}}$.
    \item \textbf{tgr\_long}: test TGR with 20\,s per assignment
          ($K=3$, \texttt{ls\_mode="full"}) to recover performance on R=20/30.
\end{enumerate}
