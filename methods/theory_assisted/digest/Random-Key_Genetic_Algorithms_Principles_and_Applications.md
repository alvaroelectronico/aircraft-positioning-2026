## Problem solved by this source

This is a reference chapter (not a paper solving a specific problem). It provides a comprehensive, self-contained exposition of the Random-Key Genetic Algorithm (RKGA) family — both the original Bean (1994) formulation and the dominant Biased Random-Key Genetic Algorithm (BRKGA) variant introduced by Gonçalves and Resende (2011). The chapter is problem-agnostic: it covers encoding, decoding, population management, crossover mechanics, and a full taxonomy of optional procedures applicable to any discrete combinatorial optimisation problem.

The scheduling application cluster is explicitly cited (refs [1, 2, 11, 32, 42, 44, 48–50, 52, 61, 87, 94, 103–105, 116, 118–121] in the scheduling bullet), confirming active use of BRKGA on scheduling and assignment problems of the type found in paper #2.

## Technique

**Core RKGA / BRKGA loop (Algorithm 1).** A population P of |P| vectors of n random keys (real numbers in [0,1)) is maintained. At each generation: (1) solutions are decoded and evaluated; (2) P is split into elite P_e (top |P_e| < |P|/2 solutions) and non-elite P_ẽ; (3) the entire elite set is copied unchanged to the next generation (elitism); (4) |P_m| mutants (fully random vectors) are injected; (5) the remaining |P| − |P_e| − |P_m| offspring are generated by parametrised uniform crossover — for each of the n genes, a biased coin with P(heads) = ρ > 0.5 is thrown; on heads the offspring inherits from the elite parent, otherwise from the non-elite parent. Key parameters: |P| ∈ [100, 500] or ⌊10×n⌋; elite fraction P_e% ∈ [0.1, 0.5]; mutant fraction P_m% ∈ [0.1, 0.5]; inheritance probability ρ ∈ (0.5, 1].

The **decoder** is the only problem-specific component. It maps a length-n random-key vector to a feasible solution and its fitness. Three decoder types: permutation (sort keys → index permutation → solution), indicator (keys divided into intervals → categorical assignments), or mixed (multi-part chromosome combining both).

**Optional procedures documented:**

- *Restart* — reinitialise entire population after I_r iterations without improvement; preserves diversity and avoids premature convergence.
- *Shake* — partial restart: keep elite set intact, fully resample non-elite chromosomes; triggered after I_s iterations without improvement; intensity parameters SK_l (lower bound) and SK_u (upper bound).
- *Island model* — p parallel populations evolved simultaneously; every g generations, i elite migrants are exchanged between islands.
- *Warm-start injection* — insert one or more good solutions (from greedy heuristics or MILP) as initial chromosomes to accelerate convergence.
- *Multi-parent crossover* — π_t total parents ranked by fitness; gene inheritance probability determined by bias function Φ(r) (options: 1/log(r+1), r⁻¹, r⁻², r⁻³, e⁻ʳ, 1/π_t). Elite parents number π_e ≤ π_t.
- *Implicit Path-Relinking (IPR)* — operates entirely within random-key space, avoiding problem-specific move operators. Two solutions selected from different populations (or from elite set) with minimum Hamming or Kendall-Tau distance md; a block of bs genes from the base solution is moved toward the guide solution; ps% of chromosome length tested per IPR call; applied every I_ipr generations.

**Parameter tuning options:** offline grid search, literature-based defaults (Table 1 gives recommended ranges), Iterated F-Race [ref 58], Adaptive BRKGA (A-BRKGA, online: updates |P|, P_e, P_m, ρ per generation using self-adaptive α, β), or BRKGA-QL (Q-Learning controller on the same parameters with ε-greedy action selection and decaying η).

**APIs:** C++ API (github.com/rfrancotoso/brkgaAPI); modernised C++ and Python API with multi-parent + IPR (github.com/ceandrade/brkga_mp_ipr_cpp and brkga_mp_ipr_python); Julia package; GPU-accelerated BRKGACuda 2.0 (github.com/discovery-unicamp/brkga-cuda).

## What transfers to paper #2

- **BRKGA as the outer evolutionary loop** — Paper #2 is NP-hard with two tightly coupled decision layers: position assignment (discrete, combinatorial) and job scheduling (continuous timing + integer κ counts). BRKGA is a natural outer loop because it is problem-agnostic at the framework level and works well on mixed assignment+ordering problems. Cost: moderate — requires designing the decoder (see below). Risk: low at the framework level.

- **Mixed-chromosome decoder design** — The chromosome should be partitioned into two segments: (a) |R| genes encoding aircraft-to-position assignment (indicator decoder: divide [0,1) into |P| equal intervals, each interval maps to one parking position); (b) |R| genes encoding the priority/start-time ordering of aircraft within each position (permutation decoder: sort these keys to get a processing order). This two-part encoding cleanly separates the assignment and sequencing decisions. Cost: moderate — the decoder must check Mode-A/B/C feasibility for every access instant and compute κ_j increments; the checker logic already exists in checker.py and can be adapted. Risk: medium — the decoder's feasibility repair for Mode-C violations (rescheduling to avoid non-interruptible job conflicts) is non-trivial and must be deterministic.

- **Permutation decoder for job sequencing within a position** — Given an assignment of aircraft to positions, the ordering of aircraft at a shared position (and the start times of their jobs) is determined by sorting the corresponding random keys. This is the canonical permutation-decoder pattern cited for scheduling problems in refs [1, 2, 48, 49] of this chapter. Cost: low once the assignment decoder is in place. Risk: low — the permutation decoder is the most mature BRKGA decoder type.

- **Warm-start injection of good initial chromosomes** — Paper #2 has a MILP baseline (milp_baseline) and a topology heuristic (topology_ms6_job_ar) already in the repo. Their solutions can be reverse-encoded into random-key vectors and injected into the initial population, providing a strong starting point. For position assignment genes: set gene_i = (assigned_position_index + 0.5) / |P|. For ordering genes: set gene_i proportional to the actual start time normalised to [0,1). Cost: low (10–20 lines). Risk: low — standard practice documented in this chapter.

- **Implicit Path-Relinking (IPR) as intensification** — IPR is problem-domain-free: it moves a block of genes from a base chromosome toward a guide chromosome, tests intermediate vectors via the decoder, and keeps the best. This is directly applicable to paper #2 without needing a problem-specific move operator on positions or schedules. The Hamming distance metric (direct IPR) is appropriate since the chromosome is a real-valued vector. Cost: low if using the provided Python API (github.com/ceandrade/brkga_mp_ipr_python). Risk: low.

- **Shake as perturbation operator** — After I_s generations without improvement, keep the elite set and resample all non-elite chromosomes. For paper #2 this is analogous to a diversification restart that preserves the best aircraft-to-position assignments found so far while exploring new orderings. Cost: trivial (built into BRKGA framework). Risk: none.

- **Island model for parallel budget utilisation** — If wall-clock time is limited but multiple CPU cores are available, running p = 2 or 3 parallel BRKGA populations with periodic elite exchange exploits parallelism. The Python API supports this. Cost: low using the API. Risk: low.

- **A-BRKGA / BRKGA-QL for parameter-free deployment** — Paper #2 instances vary in size (number of aircraft, number of positions, blocking-arc topology), making it hard to fix |P|, ρ, P_e, P_m globally. A-BRKGA (refs [4, 20, 99, 117]) adapts these online per generation. Cost: medium (requires implementing the adaptive update rules). Risk: medium — adaptive schemes can be unstable on small instances with tight time budgets.

- **Scheduling-specific decoder feasibility repair** — For paper #2, the most critical decoder step is handling Mode-C violations: if an access instant τ falls inside a non-interruptible job of the front aircraft, the schedule is infeasible and the decoder must repair it. The standard approach is to push the rear aircraft's start time forward until it falls into a Mode-A or Mode-B window. This is a deterministic earliest-feasible-start computation, consistent with the permutation decoder pattern. Cost: moderate — the repair logic is the hardest part of the decoder but is bounded in complexity by the number of jobs and blocking arcs. Risk: medium — incorrect repair can silently produce infeasible solutions that pass a lenient fitness evaluation; must cross-check against checker.py.

## What does NOT transfer

- **Continuous / real-valued optimisation use of random keys** — Some applications treat the raw key values as decision variables (e.g., parameter optimisation). Paper #2 is purely combinatorial; keys must be decoded, not used directly.

- **Indicator decoder for fine-grained timing** — Assigning exact continuous start times via indicator intervals would require an enormous chromosome and produce poor coverage of the timing space. Timing must be computed analytically by the decoder (earliest feasible start), not encoded as genes.

- **Network configuration and clustering applications** — The bulk of the scheduling references in this chapter (network configuration [68, 85, 89, 111], clustering [13, 14, 64, 117]) are structurally different from paper #2 and provide no reusable decoder patterns.

- **Crossover diversity without a good decoder** — BRKGA's population diversity mechanism is only effective if the decoder converts diverse chromosomes into diverse feasible schedules. If the decoder's repair logic is too aggressive (e.g., always snapping to the same feasible window), crossover diversity collapses. This is a design risk, not a disqualifier, but requires careful decoder engineering.

## Verdict for theory_assisted

**Priority:** High

**Rationale:** BRKGA is a mature, well-documented evolutionary framework with a proven track record on scheduling and assignment problems structurally similar to paper #2. The key insight from this chapter — that the decoder is the only problem-specific component — means the entire evolutionary machinery (elitism, biased crossover, mutants, IPR, island model, restart/shake) can be lifted directly from the existing Python API (github.com/ceandrade/brkga_mp_ipr_python) with only the decoder requiring original engineering. The two-part mixed chromosome (indicator for position assignment + permutation for sequencing) is a natural fit for the joint assignment+scheduling structure of paper #2. The most immediately actionable step is implementing the decoder with Mode-A/B/C feasibility repair and validating it against checker.py, after which the full BRKGA framework becomes available at negligible additional cost. This also complements the GRASP digest: BRKGA solutions can seed the GRASP elite pool, and conversely GRASP constructions can warm-start BRKGA populations.
