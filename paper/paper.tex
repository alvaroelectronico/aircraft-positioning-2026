\begin{abstract}
We study the \emph{aircraft hangar positioning problem} (AHPP): given a hangar
with a set of maintenance positions connected by directed \emph{blocking arcs},
a fleet of aircraft each with a known total maintenance duration, and
time-window constraints on start and finish, assign every aircraft to exactly
one position and schedule its maintenance block so as to minimise a weighted
combination of total delay, makespan, and manoeuvring movements.  The
blocking-arc structure captures physical constraints: an aircraft in a
\emph{front} position obstructs entry to and exit from a \emph{rear} position,
incurring costly movements whenever the occupancy windows of the two aircraft
overlap.  We present a compact mixed-integer linear programming (MILP)
formulation, expressed at the aircraft level so that each aircraft is treated
as a single uninterrupted maintenance block, together with two complementary
heuristic approaches: a topology-aware GRASP that steers heavy aircraft away
from high-load positions, and a fixed-assignment scheduler that re-optimises
timing given a position assignment.  Combining these in a \emph{safe pipeline}
always yields a solution at least as good as any individual component.
Computational experiments on 120 benchmark instances covering six hangar
topologies, three slack levels, and up to 30 aircraft show that the heuristic
pipeline consistently outperforms the exact solver within a 60-second budget
as soon as the fleet grows beyond the regime in which the exact solver is
competitive, while the exact solver closes the gap on the smallest instances.
\end{abstract}

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} aircraft hangar scheduling; mixed-integer linear
programming; GRASP heuristic; blocking arc constraints; maintenance planning.
