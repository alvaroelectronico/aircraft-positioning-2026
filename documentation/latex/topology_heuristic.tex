\section{Topology-Aware Heuristic}
\label{sec:topology}

\subsection{Motivation}

The constructive heuristic assigns aircraft to positions without considering
the long-term effect of the hangar topology on future flexibility.  When a
\emph{heavy} aircraft (large $D_r$) occupies a \emph{high-load} position
(one that transitively blocks many others), it holds those downstream positions
inaccessible for a long time, amplifying movements and delays in a cascade.

The topology-aware heuristic (\texttt{solvers/topology\_heuristic.py}) augments
the GRASP score with a \emph{topology penalty} that discourages placing
urgent/heavy aircraft in high-load positions.

\subsection{Topological Pre-computation}

\paragraph{Blocking load.}
For each position $p$, the \emph{blocking load} $\ell(p)$ is the number of
positions reachable from $p$ via the transitive closure of $\mathcal{B}$:
\[
    \ell(p) = \bigl|\{p' \in \mathcal{P} : p \xrightarrow{*} p'\}\bigr|
\]
Positions with $\ell(p) = 0$ block nobody (safe for heavy aircraft).
High-$\ell$ positions amplify any congestion.

The computation is a DFS per position: $O(P^2)$.

\paragraph{Aircraft slack.}
The \emph{slack} of aircraft $r$ measures its scheduling margin:
\[
    \sigma_r = \max\bigl(0,\; T_r - e_r - D_r\bigr)
\]
Small $\sigma_r$ means the aircraft is \emph{urgent}: it must start soon to
avoid delay.

\paragraph{Urgency factor.}
\[
    u_r = \frac{1}{1 + \sigma_r / \bar{\sigma}}
    \quad\in\;(0,\;1]
    \qquad \bar{\sigma} = \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r}\sigma_r
\]
$u_r \to 1$ for very urgent aircraft; $u_r \to 0$ for very relaxed ones.

\subsection{Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Topology-Aware Heuristic (multi-start)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Pre-compute $\ell(p)$ for all $p$; compute $\sigma_r$, $u_r$, $\bar{\sigma}$ \Comment{shared across starts}
\State $s^* \leftarrow \emptyset$, $z^* \leftarrow \infty$
\For{$i = 0, \ldots, \texttt{n\_starts}-1$}
    \State $\text{seed}_i \leftarrow \text{seed} + i$;\quad budget per start $\leftarrow t_{\text{limit}} / \texttt{n\_starts}$
    \While{start time budget not exhausted}
        \State Add Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\;\rho\bar{\sigma})$ to each $\sigma_r$ \Comment{$\rho=\texttt{noise\_scale\_ratio}$}
        \State Sort aircraft by $(\sigma_r + \epsilon \uparrow,\; D_r \downarrow)$ \Comment{urgency order}
        \If{LNS kick scheduled}
            \State Remove $k \in [\max(2, R/5),\; \max(3, R/3)]$ worst aircraft; re-insert
        \Else
            \State \textbf{Construction}: for each aircraft $r$ in urgency order:
            \State \quad Score: $\text{score}(r,p) = w_D \Delta_r + w_M t_f + w_V V' \cdot 2 + w_{\text{topo}} \cdot u_r \cdot \ell(p) \cdot w_D$
            \State \quad $\alpha_{\text{eff}} = \alpha + u_r(1 - \alpha)$;\quad GRASP-select position
        \EndIf
        \State $s \leftarrow \text{LightLocalSearch}(s,\;\max(5,R),\;\text{deadline})$
        \If{$z(s) < z^*$} \; $s^* \leftarrow s$; $z^* \leftarrow z(s)$ \EndIf
    \EndWhile
\EndFor
\State \Return $s^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Sequential construction.}
Unlike the constructive heuristic (which evaluates all remaining aircraft ×
positions jointly), the topology heuristic processes aircraft one at a time in
urgency order.  For each aircraft it evaluates only the $|\mathcal{P}|$
positions and selects using biased-random GRASP.  Processing in urgency order
guarantees that the most constrained aircraft choose their position while the
most desirable slots are still available.

\subsection{Topology Penalty}

The penalty added to the GRASP score when considering aircraft $r$ at position
$p$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \pi(r, p)
    = w_{\text{topo}} \cdot u_r \cdot \ell(p) \cdot w_D
    \label{eq:topo-penalty}
\end{equation}

\noindent Key properties:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Urgent aircraft} ($u_r \approx 1$): large penalty for high-load
          positions — they gravitate to $\ell(p) = 0$ slots.
    \item \textbf{Relaxed aircraft} ($u_r \approx 0$): penalty near zero — they
          can occupy any position without bias.
    \item \textbf{Zero-load positions} ($\ell(p) = 0$): penalty is always zero.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Adaptive GRASP Alpha}

The GRASP randomness is adapted per aircraft:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\text{eff}}(r) = \alpha + u_r \cdot (1 - \alpha)
    \label{eq:adaptive-alpha}
\end{equation}
Recall that $\alpha=1$ is \textbf{fully greedy} (only rank 0 selected) and
$\alpha=0$ is \textbf{uniform random} in the geometric weighting scheme
$w_i = (1-\alpha)^i$ (see Section~\ref{sec:constructive}).
Urgent aircraft (high $u_r$) drive $\alpha_{\text{eff}} \to 1$, making
selection nearly greedy — they pick the best available position with little
randomness.  Relaxed aircraft use $\alpha_{\text{eff}} \approx \alpha$,
exploring more freely.

\subsection{Construction Order and Noise Injection}

Aircraft are processed in \emph{urgency order}: smallest slack first, then
largest duration among tied-slack aircraft.  This ensures that the most
constrained aircraft choose their positions while the most desirable slots are
still available.

A small Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\; 0.005\bar{\sigma})$ is
added to each $\sigma_r$ at the start of every iteration to diversify the
ordering between iterations when aircraft have similar slack values.

\subsection{LNS Perturbation Kicks}

Every $\approx t_{\text{limit}}/8$ seconds (time-based, not iteration-count
based) the heuristic fires a \emph{perturbation kick} to escape topology-induced
local optima:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Sort the current best solution by delay DESC, then finish time DESC.
          Take a pool of $\max(k \cdot 2,\; k + 3)$ aircraft; shuffle the pool;
          select $k \in [\max(2, R/5),\; \max(3, R/3)]$ aircraft to remove.
    \item Fix the remaining $R - k$ aircraft at their current positions;
          schedule them in urgency order.
    \item Re-insert the $k$ removed aircraft using topology-aware GRASP with
          $w_{\text{topo}} = 0$ (penalty disabled), allowing the heuristic to
          explore assignments that the penalty would normally prevent — including
          patterns where heavy aircraft occupy high-load positions with precise
          timing so no blocking materialises.
\end{enumerate}

The time-based schedule ensures kicks fire with a consistent frequency
regardless of instance size.

\subsection{Bounded Local Search}

The local search (\texttt{\_light\_local\_search}) applies seven operators in
priority order, bounded by \texttt{max\_passes} $= \max(5, R)$ outer
restarts.  Candidate order is shuffled between passes to increase
neighbourhood diversity.  Any improvement restarts from operator~1.

\paragraph{Operators 1–3 (position assignment, $O(nP)$ per pass).}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Single-move}: try moving each aircraft to a different
          position; accept the first improvement.
    \item \textbf{2-opt swap}: try swapping positions of every pair of
          aircraft with different positions; accept the first improvement.
    \item \textbf{Intra-position adjacent swap}: for each position with
          $\geq 2$ aircraft, try swapping consecutive pairs in earliest-start
          order; accept the first improvement.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Operators 4–7 (intra-position sequencing, $O(n^2 P)$ per pass).}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \setcounter{enumi}{3}
    \item \textbf{Intra-position insertion}: for each position with $\geq 2$
          aircraft, try re-inserting each aircraft at every other slot in the
          same position's sequence; accept the first improvement.
    \item \textbf{Non-adjacent intra-position swap}: extend Op~3 to all
          non-consecutive pairs within the same position.
    \item \textbf{EDD repair}: for each position, evaluate three full-block
          reorderings — by earliest target finish (EDD), by slack ascending,
          and by delay/duration ratio descending — via \texttt{\_rebuild};
          accept the first improvement.
    \item \textbf{Delay-block insertion}: identify the $K = \max(2, R/10)$
          aircraft with the largest individual delay; attempt to move each
          earlier within its current position or reassign it to another
          position; accept the first improvement.
\end{enumerate}

A \emph{two-speed} mode selects the operator set based on budget per start:
\texttt{ls\_mode="fast"} (Ops~1–3 only) when budget per start is $<20$\,s,
and \texttt{ls\_mode="full"} (all operators) otherwise.  This prevents the
expensive operators from starving GRASP iterations in multi-start mode.

The \texttt{max\_passes} counter decrements only when \emph{no} operator
finds an improvement.  A deadline check after every operator prevents LS
from overshooting the time budget (fixes a runaway issue observed at
133.9\,s for a seed-8 run on R=20).

\subsection{Assignment Generator (\texttt{generate\_assignments})}
\label{sec:topo-generate-assignments}

Beyond its role as a complete solver, \texttt{TopologyHeuristic} exposes a
lightweight interface for generating diverse position assignments without
scheduling:

\begin{verbatim}
generate_assignments(instance_data, k=5, time_per_assign=2.0)
  -> list[dict[aircraft_id, position_id]]
\end{verbatim}

The method runs $k$ independent GRASP iterations, each with a budget of
\texttt{time\_per\_assign} seconds and \texttt{ls\_mode="fast"} (Ops~1–3
only), using seeds $\texttt{seed}, \texttt{seed}+1, \ldots, \texttt{seed}+k-1$.
Each call returns only the position map $\{r \mapsto p\}$; timing is discarded.

Duplicate assignments (identical position maps across seeds) are removed before
returning.  Two assignments are considered distinct if at least one aircraft
differs in its assigned position.

This interface is used by \texttt{TGRSolver} (Section~\ref{sec:tgr-solver})
to generate candidate assignments that are then scheduled exactly by
\texttt{FixedAssignmentScheduler} (Section~\ref{sec:fas}).

\paragraph{Rationale.}
Fast GRASP without heavy LS produces weaker individual solutions but explores
more diverse assignments within a fixed time budget.  The scheduling quality
of each assignment is then recovered by the exact FAS model rather than the
greedy \texttt{\_rebuild} scheduler.

\subsection{Relationship to Other Methods and Ablation Variants}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lp{9cm}}
\toprule
Configuration & Effect \\
\midrule
$w_{\text{topo}} = 0$ & Removes topology penalty; reduces to urgency-sorted GRASP
    with sequential (per-aircraft) position selection. \\
$\alpha = 1$ & Fully greedy construction (only rank~0 selected);
    kicks provide the only diversification. \\

    solver runs as pure iterated GRASP with no large-neighbourhood escape. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These three settings define the ablation study configurations
(\texttt{topology\_no\_penalty}, \texttt{topology\_greedy}, and
\texttt{topology\_no\_kicks}) available in \texttt{run\_experiments.py}.
